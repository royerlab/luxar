The seventeen supplementary videos accompanying the Luxar manuscript. Each is a screen recording of the real interactive viewer — not an offline render — at 1920×1080, 30 fps, HEVC (Main 10, 10-bit, hvc1) in an MP4 container. Every video opens with a four-second title card (number, title, one-line summary); multi-part videos carry section cards, and tours carry lower-third labels. Sixteen videos have no audio track; Video 11 carries a four-channel (quad) AAC track (the scene's own narration and ambient bed). Total 941 MB.

Playback note. These masters are 10-bit HEVC, which is archival-quality but not universally decodable in a browser: Firefox generally has no HEVC support in <video>, and Chrome's is platform- and hardware-dependent. Measured across four engines: desktop Chrome on Apple Silicon decodes them and Playwright's WebKit decodes them, but Firefox does not decode them at all (MEDIA_ERR_DECODE, zero frames rendered) and Playwright's bundled Chromium refuses with mediaError=4. Firefox nonetheless reports the correct videoWidth for a file it cannot decode — it parses the container, exposes the dimensions, then fails in the decoder — so a player can appear to have loaded and never render a frame. Note that canPlayType('video/mp4; codecs=hvc1.2.4.L120.B0') answers "probably" in Chrome, Firefox and WebKit alike, which cannot be true of all three — the browser's own claim of support is not reliable here. Download and play locally (VLC, QuickTime, mpv) if that happens. Web-friendly re-encodes are served separately from the Luxar demo site; this record holds the archival masters.

The streaming copies are not simply smaller versions of these files. Re-encoding to H.264 for browser compatibility shrinks the high-motion videos substantially but makes the slow, dark, low-bitrate ones larger: the HEVC masters reach about 1 Mbps on such material and H.264 cannot match that at equal quality. The masters here are the higher-fidelity copies in every case; consult the demo site for the current size of any streaming copy, since those are re-encoded whenever a video is re-cut.

Tribolium: contested terms, disclosed

Videos 4, 6 and 7 show level-of-detail demos built on a Tribolium castaneum light-sheet fit. That recording reaches us through Zenodo 5270323 (GIANI supplementary material) from the Cell Tracking Challenge, whose terms require the organisers' permission for public non-Challenge scientific use. Luxar's own dataset manifest marks the Tribolium demo archive redistribute: false for that reason. The authors' position, recorded here rather than left implicit, is that the restriction governs re-hosting the dataset and not a screen recording that shows it; this record redistributes no Tribolium data. A reader wanting the underlying recording should obtain it from the Cell Tracking Challenge and take the licence position from them, not from this record.
