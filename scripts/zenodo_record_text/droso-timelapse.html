A Drosophila melanogaster embryo imaged through early embryogenesis on a SiMView-type multi-view light-sheet microscope, with nuclei labelled by a histone H2A variant fusion (w; His2Av::mRFP1; +), fitted to 3D Gaussian splats per timepoint and published as a single 4D archive for the Luxar viewer.

Contents

500 timepoints spanning 4 h 10 min of development, from cellular blastoderm through gastrulation to the fully extended germband. One stacked 4D node with time as a real axis, so a viewer scrubs through development rather than loading separate files. Spatial coordinates are physical microns (Z 1.93, XY 0.40625); the time axis is in minutes.

83,221,420 splats across 500 timepoints is about 166,000 per frame. Only the timepoint being viewed is resident, so the whole-node total is not what a GPU commits: a single frame sits far below the per-node element limit. The archive is one flat node with a progressive ladder rather than a tiled or multi-level tree. On a time-stacked node the writer already orders storage by the time barrier, so spatial tiling buys nothing, and coarser whole-object levels are never selected when only one frame is ever shown.

Provenance and licence

Derived product: Gaussian-splat fits computed with the Luxar gsplat pipeline. These are lossy representations built for visualisation and interactive exploration; they are not a substitute for the source imagery in quantitative analysis. Splat counts were reduced by a cull applied using a fixed-amplitude cutoff: the archive's own statistics show a hard floor at amplitude 33.4046. The operation was intended as a cumulative-amplitude cull at retention 0.960, but the implementation in use at the time summed in float32 and tripped its threshold early, so it behaved as a fixed cutoff. Re-running a cumulative cull at 0.960 today does not reproduce this file; the fixed cutoff does.
