#### Content-tiled and batch fits resolve the background floor once, globally

`gsplat fit --tiling uniform` was built to resolve `--floor` **once** against the
whole volume and hand every tile the concrete number, precisely so independent
tile workers cannot disagree about the pedestal. Two sibling paths forwarded the
*spec* instead of a resolved *level*, so each of their workers re-estimated it on
whatever sub-volume it happened to hold. Under `--tiling content` that meant every
box estimated its own floor on its own crop: boxes are core-kept and abutting, and
the floor sets both the subtracted pedestal and the normalization range, so
neighbouring boxes were fit against different baselines and showed brightness steps
at box boundaries. Under `batch-fit` (both `run` and `submit`) it meant every
`(timepoint, channel)` task re-estimated on its own timepoint — a time-varying
pedestal, i.e. brightness flicker across the merged `kind=partition`.

Both paths now resolve the level once and hand every worker that one number.
`fit --tiling content` resolves the effective spec (`--floor`, else a `floor:` in
`--config`/the preset) against the whole volume with the "would erase all signal"
guard applied, then gives that level to the density scan, to every sequential box,
and to every `-j` box subprocess's `--floor` argv — so plan and fits share a single
basis, and a `--floor pNN` no longer forces an `np.percentile` over the entire array
(the bounded deterministic sampler is used instead, which can shift the resolved
percentile slightly on a large volume). Note what "the same level" buys and what it
does not: every box is handed the same number. (This paragraph described an interim
state, since **superseded by #1616**: a box lying above the pedestal still subtracted
its own minimum because the per-box floor was clamped **up** to `max(level, min(crop))`.
#1616 pins `image_min` to the resolved level on every path, and content boxes also share
one normalization range — see changelog.d/1616.md.) The uniform tiled path was never
affected in practice, since it subtracts the level from the raw tile *before* apodization,
whose window drives each tile's minimum to ~0.

`batch-fit` settles the open design question explicitly in favour of **one global
level for the whole timelapse**, and makes that one level safe for the dimmest
**sampled** slice by construction. It is the **minimum** of the levels resolved on a
bounded, deterministic set of at most 16 evenly spaced `(t, c)` slices spanning the
store's **full** extent: up to 4 timepoints x up to 4 channel-like coordinates,
capped *independently* so neither axis can starve the other. Concretely, `T > 1`
always samples at least two timepoints **including the endpoints `t=0` and
`t=T-1`**, and `C > 1` at least two channel-like coordinates (endpoints included).
The endpoints are what make the reduction sound: under a monotone pedestal drift
(sensor warm-up, bleaching) the run's dimmest pedestal sits at one end, so a minimum
that includes both ends is a true lower bound for the whole run. Allocating the
budget to channels *first* — a first cut of this change — made a store with 5 or more
channel-like coordinates sample exactly one timepoint, which reinstated the erase bug
in the time direction; hence time is allocated first now. The per-slice read budget
is the whole-volume budget divided by the sample count (2M voxels at the 16-slice
cap), so total voxels read stay at the single-slice budget. A minimum because the two
errors are not symmetric: under `clip(V - level, 0)` a too-LOW level is a recoverable
under-subtraction, while a too-HIGH one destroys signal — the tile worker applies the
forwarded number unguarded (by design, so no tile disagrees), so a level above some
`(t, c)`'s maximum clips that whole sub-volume to zero, which the worker legitimately
reports as an empty tile: the slice would go silently missing from the merge while
`batch-fit status` reported success. Sampling the full extent rather than the
`--timepoints`/`--channels` selection also makes the level a property of the *store*,
so a first pass over `0:50` and a resumed pass over `0:100` subtract the same
pedestal. If any sampled slice resolves to "subtract nothing" (a zero level, or the
"would erase all signal" guard refusing it), suppression is downgraded to `none` for
the whole run, loudly; each per-slice level is already guarded below its own sampled
max, so their minimum is below every sampled max and no further check is needed. It
is deliberately not resolved from the content plan's max-projection either, whose
per-voxel maximum biases the background mode upward.

**Residual risk, stated plainly:** bounded sampling can only bound the slices it
samples. A slice that is *not* sampled and dimmer than every sampled one — a
blank/bleached/bad frame between two samples of a long movie, or a channel-like
coordinate above the cap of 4 — can still be clipped to all zeros, fit 0 splats and
vanish from the merge while `status` reports success. `--floor none`, or an explicit
numeric `--floor N` low enough for the dimmest slice, is the escape hatch.

The level is recorded in the manifest as `floor_level` (documentation / inspection:
nothing reads it back) and emitted as a concrete number in every task's command.
Each sampled slice is read as a bounded sample (the whole-volume budget divided
among the samples, so plan time costs no more than before) of a **lazily** opened,
axis-pinned view, so on the normal (label-pinnable) path the floor resolution never
materializes a timepoint — not on a uniform plan, not on `submit --dry-run`, not on a
resume re-plan. (Two exceptions: the *content* box scan still materializes up to
`--plan-samples` timepoints to max-project, as before; and a store whose discovered
labels cannot be mapped onto it falls back to an eager `load_volume` per sampled
slice, announced as it happens — a correctness hedge, not the normal path.) Every
read — the floor samples and the content scan alike — pins its slice by the store's
*discovered* axis labels, so a `(T, Z, Y, X)` store **that declares its axes** (a
custom `axes` attribute or NGFF metadata) is no longer read as `CZYX` even with no
explicit `--axes`. A bare 4D zarr with no axis metadata at all still falls to the
shape heuristic, which guesses `CZYX`; pass `--axes time,z,y,x` for those. The
positional heuristic ignored `timepoint`
entirely, which made the floor measure `t=0` whatever timepoint was named *and* made
`--plan-samples` silently scan `t=0` N times instead of covering the movie. A
resumed run whose manifest predates `floor_level` keeps its recorded spec and behaves
exactly as when it was planned.

`--floor none` round-trips unchanged, and the resolved number is still subtracted
exactly once, by the same code that subtracted before — only where the number comes
from has changed. An explicit numeric `--floor 110` round-trips as that number. For
`fit --tiling content` there is one behaviour change: because the numeric now becomes
a level in the parent, which reads the volume anyway, the whole-volume "would erase
all signal" guard applies to it too, so a numeric above the sampled maximum is
reported and downgraded to `none` instead of being applied by every box. `batch-fit`
keeps forwarding a user numeric verbatim without reading anything (a numeric spec
never opens the store), exactly as before.

The complementary change is in the tile worker (`fit --tile k/M`, which is what every
uniform batch task and every `fit -j N` box subprocess runs): a *concrete numeric*
level is no longer re-guarded against the single sub-volume that worker holds. A
dim/bleached timepoint used to drop it and fall back to hard-min normalization while
every sibling task subtracted it, which is the per-timepoint pedestal difference this
change removes. **This is a behaviour change for a user numeric on that path too**:
`fit --tiling uniform --tile 3/16 --floor 110` and `fit --tiling uniform -j 4 --floor
110` (the `-j` parent forwards `--floor` verbatim) used to *warn and ignore* a level
above the volume's sampled maximum and fit anyway; they now **warn and apply** it, so
such a level windows the tile to zero and yields an empty tile. The warning is
deliberately loud and names the tile, the `(t, c)`, the level, the sampled maximum and
the consequence (0 splats, skipped by the merge), because nothing downstream of an
`.empty` marker would otherwise mention the floor. Sequential `--tiling uniform` still
guards its own single in-process resolution, as before. A volume-derived spec
(`auto`/`pNN`) is still resolved *and* guarded in the worker, since it becomes a level
for the first time there.

Bad specs (`--floor p150`, a typo, a negative `floor:` in a YAML config) are rejected
as a clean usage error *before* anything is read, on both paths (`batch-fit` at plan
time, `fit --tiling content` at the top of the content run). A *negative* resolved
level (dark-frame-corrected data) cannot be expressed as a concrete `--floor` at all,
so the SPEC is forwarded and re-resolved wherever it lands — which is not uniformly
harmless, and is not claimed to be. For `fit --tiling uniform -j N`/`--tile k/M` it is
exact: every worker re-resolves against the same whole volume with the same
deterministic sampler. For `fit --tiling content` it *degenerates to per-box
resolution*: the spec reaches `fit_gaussian_splats(crop, floor=<spec>)` per box, which
resolves it against that box crop — the same #1174 pedestal spread this change removes
elsewhere, and a violation of `fit_planned`'s own "must already be a CONCRETE level"
contract. `batch-fit` makes the same admission per task: it warns prominently that
each `(t, c)` task will resolve the spec itself (pedestals may differ across the run)
and records `floor_level=None` — "no level pinned". All three are accepted rather than
refused, because such data planned and fit fine before this change and refusing would
make it unfittable.
