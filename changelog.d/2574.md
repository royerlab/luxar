#### Mesh physical materials, Phase 3: `refract_data`, glass that refracts the data

A physical glass mesh can now refract the points, lines and splats behind it:
`add_mesh(..., material="physical", transmission=1.0, refract_data=True)`. Until now
glass refracted the background and other meshes only, and Luxar's emissive data drew
on top of it, crisp and unrefracted. With the flag the glass draws after the data it
refracts — and the data IN FRONT of the glass still lands crisp on top of it, because
the frame is partitioned by depth rather than by draw order. Absent means false, and
scenes with no physical glass render exactly as before.

The mechanism is a scene-pass split owned by the post-processing pipeline, on both
backends. The refracting glass's front faces are first rendered depth-only into one
shared depth texture; the scene is then drawn with every data material in a "behind
the glass" mode, in which each fragment compares its own depth with the glass depth at
its pixel and keeps itself only when it is behind the glass or under no glass at all;
the glass draws next (on WebGL with a screen-space quad textured with a copy of the
first pass, since three's transmission target there holds only the opaque list); and
the data is drawn once more in the complementary "in front" mode, so every data
fragment is drawn exactly once and the glass paints over nothing nearer than itself.
Emissive layers still write no depth — the classification lives in their fragment
shaders, points, lines, splats and the house mesh, GLSL and TSL alike — and picking
sees all the data as before. A refracting glass also ranks last in its `layer_order`
band, and the containment hoist no longer lifts a lens above the cluster it encloses.
Every piece of renderer state the split borrows is restored inside the same render
call, the transmission target runs at half resolution for the glass pass only on
WebGL, and an EXR capture refracts too. The frame cost is paid only while a refracting
glass is visible and is carried by the glass pass itself; the partition adds nothing
measurable on the showcase.

Building it surfaced a defect the spec had not anticipated: three copies the alpha of
what a glass samples into the glass fragment's alpha, and Luxar's HDR framebuffer alpha
is an overdraw count (8 to 18 behind a point cloud), not coverage — so a glass drawn
after the data came out thousands of times too bright, or negative, on both backends.
The house already treated that channel as undefined (the mega shader reads RGB only;
the EXR export forces alpha to 1), so the physical family now refuses it as well: the
transmitted alpha is pinned to 1 in both wrappers, a pixel no-op for Phase 2 glass and
the reason `refract_data` composites correctly. One visible consequence: an
`opaque`-mode house layer or a cutout mesh seen through glass now follows the same
rule instead of alpha-blending with its own coverage. A second finding shaped the
split: both renderers force a clear at the start of every render whose scene has a
colour background, whatever `autoClear` says, so the passes after the first run with
the background taken away.

The Layers panel gains a live "Refract data" switch on physical glass, inert with a
reason until Transmission is above 0, and the per-row Reset now restores the physical
knobs too. The showcase is an example, not a demo: `mesh_glass_lens_example.py` puts a
refracting lens, the Phase 2 default and a tinted bubble in front of a faint point
lattice — orbit behind the spheres to see the rows in front of the lens stay sharp over
it. The two physical demos moved to `packages/luxar/examples/` as
`mesh_physical_materials_example.py` and `mesh_reflections_example.py`, the latter
still the `luxar env bake` target. A Playwright spec pins both halves of the contract
on both renderers: the lens refracts the lattice behind it, and the lattice points in
front of it read the same with the lens shown or hidden.
