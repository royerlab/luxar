#### Mesh physical materials, Phase 3: `refract_data`, glass that refracts the data

A physical glass mesh can now refract the points, lines and splats behind it:
`add_mesh(..., material="physical", transmission=1.0, refract_data=True)`. Until now
glass refracted the background and other meshes only, and Luxar's emissive data drew
on top of it, crisp and unrefracted — a deliberate default, because emissive layers
write no depth and so anything IN FRONT of a glass that draws after the data is
painted over by it. That limit is unchanged and is why the new attr is opt-in, per
mesh; absent means false and every existing scene renders exactly as before.

On WebGPU the mechanism is render order alone: a refracting glass now ranks last in
its `layer_order` band (a rank even when nothing else would earn one, since the
unranked emissive layers sit at renderOrder 0), and the containment hoist no longer
lifts a lens above the cluster it encloses. On WebGL, whose transmission pass sees only
the opaque render list, the post-processing pipeline renders the scene in two passes —
everything but the glass, then only the glass plus a screen-space quad textured with a
copy of the first pass — so three's transmission target holds the data. Every piece of
renderer state the split borrows is restored inside the same render call, the
transmission target runs at half resolution for that pass only, and the frame costs
nothing measurable (0.68 vs 0.75 ms at 720p; the stock path already renders a
full-resolution transmission target for any glass in view). An EXR capture refracts
too.

Building it surfaced a defect the spec had not anticipated: three copies the alpha of
what a glass samples into the glass fragment's alpha, and Luxar's HDR framebuffer alpha
is an overdraw count (8 to 18 behind a point cloud), not coverage — so a glass drawn
after the data came out thousands of times too bright, or negative, on both backends.
The house already treated that channel as undefined (the mega shader reads RGB only;
the EXR export forces alpha to 1), so the physical family now refuses it as well: the
transmitted alpha is pinned to 1 in both wrappers, a pixel no-op for Phase 2 glass and
the reason `refract_data` composites correctly. One visible consequence: an
`opaque`-mode house layer or a cutout mesh seen through glass now follows the same
rule instead of alpha-blending with its own coverage.

The Layers panel gains a live "Refract data" switch on physical glass, inert with a
reason until Transmission is above 0, and the per-row Reset now restores the physical
knobs too. The showcase is an example, not a demo: `mesh_glass_lens_example.py` puts a
refracting lens, the Phase 2 default and a tinted bubble in front of a faint point
lattice, with an explainer card that names the limit. The two physical demos moved to
`packages/luxar/examples/` as `mesh_physical_materials_example.py` and
`mesh_reflections_example.py`, the latter still the `luxar env bake` target.
