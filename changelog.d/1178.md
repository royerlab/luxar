#### `--denoise` no longer removes a different background floor under tiling

`luxar gsplat fit --denoise` resolved the background floor from different data
depending on the tiling path. A non-tiled fit denoises the whole volume up front
and then estimates `--floor` from that denoised array, while a uniform tiled fit
(`--tiling uniform`, `--tile k/M`, `-j N`) resolved one global level from the
**raw** volume and subtracted it from tiles that had already been denoised.
Denoising collapses the noise tail and shifts the histogram mode, so the same
input, the same `--floor auto` and the same `h` removed measurably different
pedestals — a couple of intensity units on skewed-noise backgrounds, which is where
the floor matters most. Output amplitudes are background-relative, so that
difference lands directly in the fitted splats.

Estimating on denoised data is the better default: the mode estimator is more
reliable once the noise tail is collapsed. The tiled paths now resolve their
level on the denoised basis via `resolve_volume_floor_denoised`, which keeps the
whole-volume basis intact — one global level for the whole run, the invariant
#1174 established — and applies the denoise-induced *correction* measured on a
small bounded probe: a few evenly spaced, roughly cubic, contiguous crops
totalling at most `DENOISE_PROBE_BUDGET_VOXELS` (2M) voxels, denoised with the
tiles' exact parameters. The level moves by the shift the same estimator sees
between the raw and denoised probe. When the volume fits the probe budget the
probe *is* the whole volume, so the corrected level equals the
denoised-whole-volume estimate exactly and the two paths agree bit for bit;
above it, the correction is exact to the extent that the shift is spatially
uniform.

Above the budget the probe and the floor sample no longer describe the same
population — a few cubic crops from the middle versus slabs spanning the whole
volume — and the estimators are non-linear, so a probe can propose a shift that
moves the level *away* from the non-tiled reference. The messenger therefore has
to agree on the baseline before its shift is trusted: when the probe's own raw
level differs from the whole-volume raw level by more than the shift it proposes,
the correction is declined with a note naming both levels and the shift, and the
raw-basis level is kept — i.e. exactly the previous behaviour for that volume. A
whole-volume probe disagrees by exactly zero, so the exact case is never
declined.

The probe is a pure function of the volume's shape and the budget, so a
`--tile k/M` subprocess, a `-j N` worker and the sequential path all reach the
same level without coordinating, as they already did for the raw level and the
shared normalization range — provided they share a denoise backend as well as
`h`, since `--denoise-backend auto` picks skimage on a CPU-only host and the
torch kernel on a GPU one, and the two differ slightly. The probe costs one extra
denoise pass, bounded by the probe budget, per *resolution*: once for a
sequential tiled fit, but once per worker on the parallel paths, where every
`-j N` / `--tile k/M` worker resolves its own level — and for a volume within the
probe budget that means one whole-volume denoise pass per worker. Nothing changes
with `--denoise` off, and a numeric or `none` `--floor` is a user absolute that is
never corrected. An unavailable NLM backend or any raising denoiser prints a note
and keeps the raw-basis level rather than failing the fit.

The "would erase all signal" guard is re-applied to the corrected level against
the same bounded raw sample the uncorrected level is judged on, not against the
probe: NLM only shrinks the range, and a center-cropped probe can legitimately
see no signal at all (a masked or zero-padded middle), which would otherwise veto
a level the raw path applies.

`--tiling content` still ignores `--denoise` altogether (warned about at
startup). `batch-fit` under `--denoise` keeps a raw-basis level in both of its
modes — the plan resolves it from the raw input before the denoise job runs, and
the on-the-fly mode hands the tasks that number — which is out of scope here and
tracked separately.
