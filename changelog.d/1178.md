#### `--denoise` no longer removes a different background floor under tiling

`luxar gsplat fit --denoise` resolved the background floor from different data
depending on the tiling path. A non-tiled fit denoises the whole volume up front
and then estimates `--floor` from that denoised array, while a uniform tiled fit
(`--tiling uniform`, `--tile k/M`, `-j N`) resolved one global level from the
**raw** volume and subtracted it from tiles that had already been denoised.
Denoising collapses the noise tail and shifts the histogram mode, so the same
input, the same `--floor auto` and the same `h` removed measurably different
pedestals — a couple of intensity units on skewed-noise backgrounds, which is where
the floor matters most. Output amplitudes are background-relative, so that
difference lands directly in the fitted splats.

Estimating on denoised data is the better default: the mode estimator is more
reliable once the noise tail is collapsed. The tiled paths now resolve their
level on the denoised basis via `resolve_volume_floor_denoised`, which keeps the
whole-volume basis intact — one global level for the whole run, the invariant
#1174 established — and applies the denoise-induced *correction* measured on a
small bounded probe: a few evenly spaced, roughly cubic, contiguous crops
totalling at most `DENOISE_PROBE_BUDGET_VOXELS` (2M) voxels, denoised with the
tiles' exact parameters. The level moves by the shift the same estimator sees
between the raw and denoised probe. When the volume fits the probe budget the
probe *is* the whole volume, so the corrected level equals the
denoised-whole-volume estimate exactly and the two paths agree bit for bit.

Above the budget the probe is a crop of the middle of the volume, and whether its
shift can be believed depends on which estimator measured it. Measured on
synthetic stacks with a known pedestal — six background families (flat Gaussian,
Poisson, gamma-skewed, an axial gradient, a vignette, a masked middle) against the
level `--tiling none` computes — a percentile floor lands 1.4 intensity units from
the non-tiled estimate on average where the uncorrected level averaged 4.4 units
off and was as much as 11.7 off, it is closer on 28 of the 36 volumes, and it
keeps improving as the probe grows (on a 12-volume subset: 1.03, 0.98, 0.59, 0.38
units at 2.3, 4.7, 18.8, 37.5% of the volume). The `auto` histogram-mode shift does neither: it
helped on the two families whose true shift was large and hurt on the four whose
true shift was small, it ended up closer on only 21 of 36 volumes, and growing the
probe from 2% of the volume to 37% did not improve it at all (0.91, 0.75, 0.72,
0.90 — and its worst case got worse). A real light-sheet stack is nearer 0.03%
than to anything measured here. So above the probe budget the
correction is applied for a `pNN` floor and **not** for `auto`: the default keeps
its raw-basis level — exactly the previous behaviour for that volume — and prints
one note saying so and pointing at `--floor pNN` or a non-tiled fit. No probe is
denoised in that case, so the skip costs nothing rather than an NLM pass. This
replaces a first attempt at judging the shift by whether the probe agreed with the
whole volume about the *raw* level, which was backwards: it grew more permissive
the larger the proposed shift, and it refused percentile corrections that were
large, correct improvements.

The probe is a pure function of the volume's shape and the budget, so a
`--tile k/M` subprocess, a `-j N` worker and the sequential path all reach the
same level without coordinating, as they already did for the raw level and the
shared normalization range — provided they share a denoise backend as well as
`h`. `--denoise-backend auto` picks skimage on a CPU-only host and the torch
kernel on a GPU one, and the two are measurably different: on synthetic pedestals
their outputs differed by a mean of ~0.4 and by tens of intensity units at
individual voxels, and the resolved `auto` level came out different in every
configuration tried (by 0.001–0.043 units here, and up to 0.085 on a reviewer's
data). Pin `--denoise-backend` for a fleet spanning heterogeneous hosts. Where
there is a probe at all it costs one extra denoise pass, bounded by the probe
budget, per *resolution*: once for a sequential tiled fit,
but once per worker on the parallel paths, and for a volume within the probe
budget that means one whole-volume denoise pass per worker. Nothing changes with
`--denoise` off — including the log surface — and a numeric or `none` `--floor` is
a user absolute that is never corrected. An unreadable probe, an unavailable NLM
backend, any raising denoiser, and a probe that emits non-finite values all print
a note and keep the raw-basis level rather than failing the fit or handing the
tiles a NaN level.

The "would erase all signal" guard is re-applied to the corrected level against
the same bounded raw sample the uncorrected level is judged on, not against the
probe. Not because the probe would catch less — NLM shrinks the range, so the
probe's denoised max is a *tighter* bound and would veto more — but because a
centre-cropped, smoothed probe can legitimately see no signal at all (a masked or
zero-padded middle), and vetoing there drops floor suppression for a whole run.
The cost of that choice is a gap: a level between the denoised and the raw maximum
is not caught. A background-mode level does not land there in practice.

`--tiling content` still ignores `--denoise` altogether (warned about at
startup). `batch-fit` under `--denoise` keeps a raw-basis level in both of its
modes — the plan resolves it from the raw input before the denoise job runs, and
the on-the-fly mode hands the tasks that number — which is out of scope here and
tracked separately.
