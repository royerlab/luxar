#### One step quantum per dimension: a Step override, and wheel stepping on the sliders

Animating a continuous dimension used to ignore its authored step entirely —
the play button advanced by a tenth-of-the-range-per-second pace scaled by
FPS (the 4D hypersphere's W stepped 0.3 while `[`/`]` moved 0.5 and a slider
notch 0.03). The per-dimension right-click menu now has a Step section
alongside Speed and Loop Mode: Auto (the historical behavior), preset
multipliers of the dimension's base step with their computed values, and a
custom field. The chosen step drives both the animation tick and the `[`/`]`
keys, so the playback quantum is decoupled from the playback rate — FPS only
decides how often a step lands. On a discrete dimension the override is
quantized to the authored grid (one cell minimum), so a sub-cell quantum can
never freeze playback against the value snap, and the preset list offers only
whole-cell multipliers there.

The sliders also gain mouse-wheel stepping: one base step per notch
(the authored step, else 1% of the range), Shift for fine tenth-steps,
Ctrl for coarse ×10 jumps, and Ctrl+Shift for extra-fine hundredth-steps,
clamped at the range ends.
The wheel deliberately ignores the animation override — hand stepping stays
on the dimension's own grid — and slider dragging is unchanged. Wheel
stepping reads whichever scroll axis carries the motion, so Shift+wheel on
a standard mouse (which browsers deliver as a horizontal scroll) steps in
the scrolled direction instead of always stepping down; the Layers panel's
range sliders and the GUI number sliders (rendering controls) get the same
axis fix. The GUI number sliders also drop their inverted modifier
convention (Shift used to mean coarse there): relative to their
1/10th-step base, Shift is now finer, Ctrl coarse, Ctrl+Shift finest —
matching every other slider. And a discrete dimension's value snap is now
range-aware, so an off-grid range end can no longer snap the playhead
outside the data, and the t+1 prefetch predicts exactly the snapped value
a loop wrap lands on.
