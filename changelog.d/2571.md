#### Scene environments for physical meshes: the data is the light (Phase 4)

A physical mesh is lit by an environment, and until now that environment was
always three's neutral room. `viewer_config.environment` picks the source:
`room` (the default, unchanged), `hdri` (an equirectangular image, `url`), or
`scene`, in which case the viewer captures a cube map from a probe inside the
scene itself — the centre of the bounds, a node's bbox centre (`node:<path>`) or
a literal `(x, y, z)` — so a chrome sphere reflects the point cloud around it and
a glass shell tints the data behind it. The capture hides the physical meshes for
its six draws, is redone when geometry commits, a slice moves or an appearance
changes, only once the loader has settled, and never per frame or on camera
motion; both backends re-prefilter the cube in place. `resolution` (16 to 1024,
default 128) and `intensity` complete the config.

A published scene should not pay for a live capture on every visit, so the
capture can be baked. `luxar env bake scene.luxar.zarr` serves the store and the
built viewer from one process, drives a headless browser through the viewer's
`?bakeEnv` one-shot (`&probe=` and `&envResolution=` override the authored
values) and attaches the result; `luxar env attach` writes a capture the bake
produced elsewhere. The map lives in a root-level `environment/` sidecar group
that no node listing sees, as a `(6, H, W, 4)` array of half-float bits under a
digest-derived name, and it carries the `content_hash` of the scene it was baked
from: a map whose scene has since changed is reported and ignored in favour of
the live capture. The group is excluded from the scene's own `content_hash`, so
attaching a map never invalidates a visitor's warm cache and attaching the same
map twice writes nothing.

`demo_mesh_reflections` — an emissive swirl with a chrome and a glass sphere,
`environment.source="scene"` — is the showcase and the bake target. Baked and
live agree at SSIM 0.9985 (WebGL) and 0.9989 (WebGPU) on the same backend; the
two backends agree at 1.000 on a baked map and 0.986 on live capture. House
materials never read the environment, so scenes without a physical mesh render
exactly as before.
