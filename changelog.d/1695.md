#### An offline turntable no longer bakes coarse-LOD pops into the exported sequence

Since the rAF loop began running for the whole offline capture, the depth-sort
scheduler and the LOD group selector correctly follow the orbiting camera — but
that also leaves the auto-LOD selector live for the entire sweep, and it is
frustum-aware. On a `tiles` / `adaptive` / `overview` gsplat scene, a tile whose
world bbox leaves the frustum mid-orbit is demoted to its coarsest ready level,
and the resident-byte budget may release its fine one. When it swings back into
view the fine level reloads asynchronously — but `OfflineCaptureStrategy` took
exactly one `requestAnimationFrame` per exported frame and never waited for
readiness, so those frames went into the ZIP/MP4 at the coarse level and popped
back a few frames later.

The capture loop now waits. After each frame's orbit callback is removed (the
camera pose is fixed from that point, so the extra frames only let pending loads
land — draining any earlier would keep orbiting while waiting), it spends extra
rAF ticks until every in-frame LOD group is showing its selected level at final
quality. That predicate is the new `LODGroupRegistry.isCaptureQuiescent()`: it
skips off-screen entries (held coarse on purpose, drawing nothing) and entries
that are not effectively visible (a layer toggled off, or authored
`visible=false`, anywhere up the ancestor chain — those cannot start a deferred
load at all, while the frustum-only selector still records a fine level for
them, so blocking on one would never resolve), and otherwise
requires that the displayed level equals the aspiration, that the aspiration is
ready, fresh for the current view version and done streaming its additive
ladder, and that no child is loading. "Done streaming" is shape-aware: a leaf
answers with its `hasMoreLODs()` thunk, while a deferred group child (the
`overview` recipe's fine `kind=partition` branch, which has no such thunk)
answers with the folded `committedLadderComplete` of its visible stamped
leaves — those part leaves are at chunk-1 the instant the branch becomes ready,
so a leaf-only check would have released the frame on exactly the recipe this
issue is about. It also requires that the selector is not
still _wanting_ a different level — a new `LODGroupEntry.desiredChildIndex` records
what the selector picked before the ready gates get a say, which closes a real
one-frame hole: `activeChildIndex` only ever advances onto a READY level, so in
the frame where a lazy level's load lands (`ready` set, `loading` cleared) the
swap has not happened yet, and a predicate reading only ready/loading/displayed
would call that window settled and film the coarse level. A `desired` level that
has `failed` deliberately does not block, since it can never become ready this
frame.

The first of those extra ticks is mandatory rather than part of the wait, for a
per-frame-callback-ordering reason. `AnimationController.animate` runs
`controls.update()`, then every per-frame callback in Map insertion order, then
the render. The LOD selector is registered once at pipeline init while the
capture removes and re-adds its orbit callback on every iteration — so the orbit
callback is always last, and `applyOrbitRotation` moves the camera
synchronously. Inside the single frame the loop awaits for frame N, the selector
therefore evaluates pose N−1 and only afterwards does the camera advance to pose
N. Polling straight away would read state computed for the previous pose, and on
the exact frame a tile re-enters the frustum the selector has not seen the
re-entry yet — the predicate would report settled and that frame would still be
filmed coarse, one popped frame per re-entry. One more tick puts the selector on
the pose being captured. The cost is one rAF per frame even on a fully settled
scene that has LOD groups; against a full render plus an async GPU readback plus
an encode per frame, ~16 ms is noise.

A scene with no LOD at all pays nothing, not even that tick. The settle hook is
tri-state — `true` / `false` / `null` — and the provider wired at init returns
`null` when there is no scene loader, no LOD registry, or a registry with zero
registered `lod_group`s. `null` means "no `lod_group` to wait for" and skips the
drain outright, which matters because the provider is wired unconditionally:
without the third state, a plain points/lines capture would have started paying
the catch-up frame for a selector it does not have. That answer is deliberately
narrow, and narrower than "nothing here can be mid-load": a `--recipe stream`
scene (one leaf with an additive ladder, no `lod_group`) answers `null` while
its progressive refinement is still climbing the ladder, so its early frames can
still be exported at a partial prefix — the same artifact class, out of scope
here. The hook is read through a `try`/`catch` as well (the treatment
`AnimationController.pacingSuspended()` already gives its injected predicate),
so a throwing provider degrades to "do not wait" instead of being caught by the
loop's outer handler and discarding the sequence as "Recording failed".

The wait is bounded by both a 2 s deadline (on the monotonic
`performance.now()`, so an NTP/DST step cannot move it backwards) and a
120-frame cap (inclusive of that catch-up tick), whichever trips first — the
frame cap is not redundant, since under a stubbed clock (unit tests, fake
timers) the millisecond deadline alone would spin forever. After three
consecutive timeouts the loop pauses draining and warns once, so a scene that
can never settle (resident-byte thrash on an over-budget partition) cannot
silently multiply the capture's wall-clock by the timeout on every remaining
frame. That pause re-arms: a paused frame still spends a free, non-waiting probe
of the predicate (no rAF, no poll, so it costs exactly what an undrained frame
did) and resumes draining the moment the probe reports the scene settled. A
terminal pause would have made the fix inert on precisely the scenes it targets
— an over-budget `adaptive` / `overview` partition, or a Capture pressed before
the initial load finished, burns the budget on frames 0–2, pauses at frame 3,
and would export the remaining hundreds of frames with the old behaviour even
though the scene settles seconds later.

Any timeouts at all are reported at the end of the run with a warning and a
toast, so a degraded sequence is visible rather than a mystery — an exact "N of
M frames" (M = the frames the loop attempted) when waiting stayed on for the
whole run, and otherwise a statement that waiting was paused after three
consecutive timeouts and the frames captured while it was off were taken without
it. The count cannot be quoted in that second case: it only counts the frames
that waited and gave up, while everything captured with the wait off is
uncounted, so it badly understates the damage. (It is not pinned at three —
three bounds the consecutive streak, and a run alternating timeout/settle can
total more before three land in a row.) Both reports say the frames may not show
the level the selector had settled on, rather than that they are coarse: the
predicate is direction-blind, and `displayed !== active` also fires while the
never-downgrade gate legitimately holds a _finer_ previously-displayed level
over a coarser aspiration that is still streaming.

Waiting rather than forcing the finest level is the deliberate choice: a capture
visits the whole scene, so pinning finest across a tiled partition would make
peak residency the entire dataset, which is exactly what the resident-byte
budget exists to prevent. Waiting costs time, not memory. The distance-driven
coverage cross-fade is deliberately left alone — its weight is a function of
projected bbox area rather than wall-clock, so it already spreads smoothly over
consecutive exported frames, and draining it would turn a dissolve into a hard
cut.
