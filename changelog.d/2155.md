#### Ocean currents: bound what the opening view keeps resident, and stop losing a clamp in the log

The ocean-currents demo kept **every element in the scene resident whenever the
globe was on screen** — 19,440,000 of them at the authored opening pose,
measured in the browser. `partition=` (added in #1104) bounds how big a single
node may be, which is what stopped the North Atlantic going missing, but it does
not bound residency: every part is fetched and drawn and the GPU only
frustum-culls, and at a whole-globe framing nothing is outside the frustum.

Both layers are now a `kind=partition` of 16 per-tile `kind=lod` ladders. A
non-default level is cheap-attached with an `ensureLoaded` thunk, so a tile that
is far away or off-screen never fetches its fine data. At the same authored
pose: **5.47M resident, down from 19.44M**, with 15 of 16 tiles per layer on
their coarsest level and the front-facing tile one level finer.

The ladders are hand-built rather than requested through `substitutive_lod=`,
which coarsens by synthesising gsplats — right for a density cloud, wrong where
the ribbons are the picture. Coarse levels are fewer *whole* elements, and each
carries the ink of the level it replaces: points take a `sqrt` radius (spacing
on a sphere goes as `1/sqrt(N)`) plus a margin for the gaps a random subset
leaves past the mean, ribbons take a linear width (the line shader floors
sub-pixel lines at 1.5px and dims by `pixelWidth/1.5`, so ink goes as
`count × width`). Ribbon lift is now derived from the COARSEST globe radius
rather than the finest, so a tile dropping a level can no longer bury the
ribbons above it in the shell.

The ladder is deliberately shallow. At a compression of 4 the numbers are much
better — 1.52M resident, 12.8x — and the opening shot is visibly worse: a 1/16
globe renders as a mottled shell of separable discs. The cost of choosing the
picture is on disk: ~546 MB against ~290 MB, since substitutive LOD stores
levels rather than deltas.

Separately, and behind all of the above: an element-texture clamp is silent data
loss, and the viewer was easy to miss saying so. The "already reported" flag was
keyed on the layout OBJECT, so with two oversized nodes of the same geometry
type only the FIRST was ever announced and the second's tail vanished without a
word. It is now keyed per distinct offender, reported at error level rather than
warning, and says how many elements will never render and that they are one
contiguous region of the scene.
