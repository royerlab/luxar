#### A timelapse step no longer flashes through the coarsest LOD

Stepping the Time slider on a 4D substitutive LOD group dropped the display from
the finest level to the **coarsest** and climbed back, on every step. Measured on
a 151-timepoint gsplat timelapse: 6,900 splats → 108 → 6,900 in ~70 ms, four
frames at 1.6% of the geometry — so playing the axis was a strobe, even though
the fine level's data for the new timepoint was already cached (the 70 ms is
decode, not fetch).

The registry's slice-aware fallback shows the coarsest fresh level the instant a
scrub invalidates the aspiration, so the new slice appears immediately at low
detail. That is right when the aspiration is seconds away and wrong when it is
milliseconds away — and the coarse level wins the race precisely because it
carries almost nothing. The never-downgrade gate could not help: its rule is that
staleness beats quality, so what is on screen is released the moment the view
version moves.

The registry now holds the previous slice instead, but only when the fresh
fallback is below half the committed elements of what is already displayed — not
a coarser view of the new slice but a token of it. For a timelapse step the held
frame is the previous frame, which is what a video player leaves up while the
next one decodes. The hold is bounded to 250 ms, spent from when it starts and
not refreshed by later version bumps, so a continuous drag still degrades to live
coarse geometry exactly as before.
