#### `reencode` re-quantizes centers too, and the skills learn how to tune a fit

`luxar gsplat reencode` has been documented everywhere as changing "only the
on-disk Cholesky encoding". It does not: `write_gsplats_tree` re-encodes the
centers with the same mode, and for a coordinate column `auto` and `memory` both
mean per-axis uint16 fixed-point (float32 only once an axis spans 2**16). So a
`reencode -e memory` — the command's own default — re-quantizes positions, and
centers are bit-exact only under `precision`. That matters most on a stacked
axis, where a coordinate off by one quantization step lands ~150 sigma from its
own slice and the slice renders as nothing. The claim is corrected in the
command's help, the CLI README, the format/migration guide and the gsplat-edit
skill.

`batch-fit`'s `--cull-retention` help still promised "0.95 for uniform tiles,
0.999 for content boxes". Every batch task is a `fit --preset <the run's
preset>` and every preset sets 0.999, so both modes have been near-lossless for
a while; the help now says so.

The skills gain the fit-tuning material that was written for them but never
reached this branch: a symptom-to-knob index over the measured sections, how to
read `fit`'s stamped foreground PSNR against `compare`'s, what `--seeds`
actually proposes once the post-fit amplitude cull has disposed, the two
microscopy containers that do and do not load (`.lsm` eagerly, `.h5j` not at
all), the fixed `transform` order and its pre-rotation frame, and how a demo's
generated data manifest is meant to be regenerated rather than hand-edited.
