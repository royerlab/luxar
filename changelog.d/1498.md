#### The LOD method flags are named symmetrically: `--add-method` / `--subst-method` (#1498)

`gsplat lod` took `-m/--method` for the **additive** ordering but
`--substitutive-method` for the level-replacing reduction — one qualified, one
bare — so the unqualified name silently meant "additive" across every gsplat
surface. Meanwhile `luxar mesh lod` used that same bare `--method` for its
**decimation** algorithm. Two commands, one flag name, two different mechanisms,
and no way to tell from the name which one you were setting.

Both halves are now qualified and neither is bare: `-m/--add-method` and
`--subst-method`, with `--merge-add-method` / `--merge-subst-method` on the
`batch-fit` merge. `mesh lod`'s decimation knob becomes `--subst-method` (it
selects the substitutive reduction, as on `gsplat lod`, even though the
algorithms differ) and gives up its `-m`, which is reserved for the additive
ordering mesh will gain with its own ladder. Old spellings are refused with a
pointer naming the replacement and carrying your value, rather than silently
falling back to a flag's default and doing different work than you asked for.

Batch manifests written before the rename keep working. They persist these knobs
under dash-less keys, which looks like a vocabulary independent of the flags —
but the sbatch generator turns each stored key straight into a flag, so a
pre-rename manifest would have emitted `--substitutive-method` into its merge
command and every resumed Slurm merge job would have died on an unknown option.
The translation is applied at both ends: when a stored plan is read, and when the
merge command is emitted.
