#### Oversized gallery scenes now stream in bounded commits

The remaining large point and line leaves in the gallery demos now carry explicit additive ladders, including slice-aware opening rungs for animated and categorical scenes. The 44-million-point 4D fractal is written as bounded 150,000-point parts instead, because a single sliced ladder cannot satisfy both the opening-share floor and the per-commit ceiling. These scenes now paint progressively rather than asking the browser to decode and commit hundreds of thousands to millions of elements at once.

#### Per-node ladder decisions for the last three gallery demo leaves

Laddering the gallery corpus left three leaves that `scripts/check_demo_ladders.py`
still fails, each for a different reason, and each needing an authored answer
rather than a corpus-wide sweep.

`exotic_surfaces` has two family nodes on a hidden two-stop categorical axis, and
each node carries only its own coordinate — so `hidden_axis_stops` reports 1,
`stream_ladder` skips its sliced share floor, and rung 0 stays at the unsliced
39,062-element download budget: 4.56% of `Minimal surfaces` (857,226 points) and
8.42% of `Algebraic surfaces` (464,073), both under the gate's 10% rung-0 share
floor. Nothing is *divided* on a one-stop node, but the reset the share contract
exists for is real — stepping `family` swaps the whole wall and the incoming node
restarts at rung 0 — so the demo now applies the `n/8` share floor itself, in a
`family_ladder` helper, and keeps the **unrelaxed** 900,000-element commit
ceiling. Rung 0 becomes 107,154 and 58,010 points at 12.50% each, with largest
increments of 428,610 and 232,033, inside both the 0.6 degeneracy bound and the
1,000,000 absolute commit cap. The floor is applied by hand rather than by
`slices=2` because `stream_ladder` reads `slices` as a divisor for the commit
ceiling as well as a predicate for the floor, and a node with no second slice
should not get the doubled 1,800,000 ceiling: inert at the authored
`RESOLUTION = 112`, but counts scale about as `resolution**2` (measured: 2,023,609
points at 172) and the relaxed ceiling authors a 1,011,801-element increment
there, over the gate's cap, where the unrelaxed one stops at 900,000. The cost of
the share floor is accepted rather than hidden: first paint on `Minimal surfaces`
goes from 39,062 to 107,154 points, i.e. from the policy's 200 ms budget to 549 ms
at its own 25 Mbps and 16 B/element assumptions (Algebraic: 200 -> 297 ms). What
it buys is the framing after a `family` step — 12.5% of the incoming wall rather
than 4.56%.

`galaxy_simulation` fails the other arm. Its five age-bin layers are wildly
uneven — 110 / 966 / 12,455 / 44,569 / 41,900 stars, each present in all 241
frames — and a share-sized rung is the wrong currency for the thin ones. At
232,806 rows, `Disc 50-300 Myr` is only 16% over the 200,000 threshold at which a
ladder becomes mandatory, so its rung stayed at the 39,062 budget and spread to a
measured 144 rows at the sparsest 5th-percentile frame, under the 250-element
absolute first-paint floor. Every played layer now floors rung 0 at a per-FRAME
budget of 300 rows as well (300 rather than 250 because the seeded random prefix
is a multinomial over frames, whose p05 frame lands ~8-11% below the mean —
measured 144 against a 162 mean), re-resolving the same capped schedule around
whichever rung 0 is larger. `Disc 50-300 Myr` becomes three rungs of 72,300 /
72,300 / 88,206 with a measured p05 frame of 275, and every other node of the
default build is byte-for-byte unchanged, because its `n/8` share rung already
wins the `max()`. The floor is passed on the HII and bulge layers too even though
it does not bind there at the authored star count (p05 frames of 343 and 2,423),
because both populations are derived from `--stars`: HII is the youngest 3% of the
disc, so `--stars 50000` leaves 361,500 rows whose share rung spreads to a
measured p05 frame of 166 and fails the gate, against 274 with the floor. The
floor is also clamped to the gate's own 0.6 degeneracy bound on any node above its
200,000-row audit threshold, because an absolute per-frame budget is a share of
`stars_in_bin` rather than of the node: at `--stars 250000 --frames 900` the
242-star `< 50 Myr` layer would take a 270,000-row floor over 217,800 rows and
resolve to a flat leaf, and at `--stars 40000 --frames 600` the 405-star
`50-300 Myr` layer would put 74% of the node in rung 0. Merging the thin bins was
rejected: the five-way age split is the comparison the demo exists for, and the
merged 1,076 stars would still need ~28% of the node in rung 0.

Also corrected a false arithmetic claim in the `demo_4d_fractals.py` partition
justification. It said a slice-aware ladder's 5,558,655-point first rung "cannot
fit the 900,000-point commit ceiling"; since the sliced commit cap became
resident-aware the ceiling for 300 stops is 270,000,000 and `stream_ladder`
resolves that ladder without complaint. The real reason no single leaf works is
that the gate's two arms are jointly unsatisfiable there: rung 0 must be at least
10% of the node (4,446,924 points), while the absolute commit cap's
per-coordinate relaxation needs *exactly one* slice dim and this leaf has two
(`fractal`, `w`), leaving the whole node-level 1,000,000 cap in force. The 297
partition children are cut by array INDEX at the authored 150,000-element cap
rather than per plane — measured, 221 of them span more than one `(fractal, w)`
coordinate — and at 69,233 to 150,000 points each they fall under the gate's
200,000 threshold outright. The justification also records the trade that shape
takes: roughly 30 laddered parts of ~1,482,308 clear every arm as well, and 297
parts cost about one request per part to bootstrap.
