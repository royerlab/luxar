#### The nav, spatial-query and WebGL-error E2E waits stop returning on unread state (#1726)

Two of the E2E poll loops ended their budget by simply returning:
`waitForNavigationComplete` closed with `// Timeout is not an error - loading may
have completed` and `waitForSpatialQuery` with the same shape, and every
iteration of both swallowed a probe failure in an empty `catch`. That is right
for the case they were written for — a navigation or query that settled before
polling started, which is exactly why several call sites deliberately choose the
silent variant, their action never toggling `isLoading` at all. It is
indistinguishable, though, from a run in which every probe FAILED:
`getLuxarState` rejects on a page with no debug interface, on a destroyed
execution context, and — since it gained a deadline of its own (#1651 / #1724) —
on a page too starved to answer within 45 s; the loop swallowed each rejection
and returned after its full budget having read no viewer state at all.

What that cost at today's call sites is ATTRIBUTION, not a false pass. All nine
`waitForNavigationComplete` calls follow the wait with a page probe of their own —
`getLuxarState`, a bare `page.evaluate`, or a `getDimensionValue` that answers
`-1` and then fails its own `expect` — so a helper that read nothing produced a
FAILING test one statement later, charged to the wrong statement and reported as a
bare `Debug interface not ready` or a nonsense `-1`. Six of the nine (all in
`spatial-index-accuracy.spec.ts`) additionally sit right after
`waitForSpatialQueryOrThrow`, which cannot have succeeded unless the page answered
within the last 8 s, so on those the new throw is near-unreachable and what they
get is the attributed warn. The vacuous pass proper — confirmed nothing, and
nobody re-probes afterwards — is a hole in the CONTRACT rather than a bug in a
current caller, and it is closed here for the next caller alongside the
attribution win, which is the immediate one.

The end of the budget is now a real decision, split on one question: did any
probe ever answer? If none did, the helper throws, naming itself, its nominal
budget, the time it actually spent, how many probes went out and how many of them
yielded no usable state (a rejection, or an answer the condition could not be read
off), and the last probe failure — it never once read viewer state, so there is
nothing for the caller's assertions to stand on. If a probe answered but the
condition never settled, the helper still returns normally — the tolerated case
those nine call sites depend on — but emits exactly one `console.warn` in the
house `[⚠️] [E2E <helper>]` form, with the same counts and the carried probe
diagnostic appended in the shared `describeLastProbeError` wording the two loops
that already throw (`waitForPointsLoaded`, `waitForDimensionNavigation`) use. Both
reports are composed by one module-private helper next to it, since the two loops'
messages differ only in their names and nouns, so a single grep still finds every
carried-failure report. Both loops clear that record on an answered probe, as
those siblings do: a missing debug interface during initialization is normal and
recovers, and carrying that first failure to the end would blame it for a loop
that then polled a healthy page. The COUNTS are what survives the clearing —
without them a 15 s window in which 149 of its ~150 probes rejected and only the
last one answered would report as a clean "answered but never settled" on the
strength of that single read. Both reports also state the measured elapsed time
next to the nominal budget, because neither loop clamps its probes: that is
deliberate — these loops succeed on ANY satisfying answer, so a clamp would fail a
healthy-but-slow page — but it means a `waitForNavigationComplete(page, 15000)`
whose preliminary wait spends 10 s and whose probe then burns its own 45 s
deadline runs for ~55 s, and reporting that as "15000ms" would send the reader
after the wrong problem. A non-positive budget needs no special case: it never
enters `waitForSpatialQuery`'s loop, and the counts then report "0 probe(s)
attempted" rather than implying probes that were tried and came back empty. The
healthy path is unchanged and stays silent: those call sites succeed on their
first probe, `isLoading` already being false when they poll.

`waitForNavigationComplete` also had a second silent exit. Its preliminary
`page.waitForFunction` for `typeof isLoading === 'boolean'` used to `catch`, sleep
300 ms and return, on the stated reasoning that "navigation completes before we
start polling" — which does not explain a missing `isLoading` at all, since the
flag exists whenever the debug interface does. That `catch` now falls through into
the poll loop, so the state probe gets its chance to answer or to produce a better
diagnostic, and the single end-of-budget decision governs both paths. The
preliminary failure is named in the answered-but-unsettled warn with the time it
cost — "the flag was unreadable for the first 10 s" is exactly what explains a
15 s budget that never saw `isLoading` clear, though that figure is
deterministically the clamped preliminary budget rather than a measurement of
anything — but it is not a probe failure: it stays out of the probe counts, and out
of the throw, where the carried probe rejection is both fresher and the better
headline. The loop
became a `do`/`while` so it always probes at least once: the preliminary wait can
cost up to 10 s on its own, and reporting "no probe answered" without having
probed would be a false diagnostic. Its `min(timeout, 10000)` ceiling is now
floored at 1 ms as well, because Playwright reads `timeout: 0` as NO timeout — a
non-positive budget used to park that wait for the whole test, the exact opposite
of what the ceiling is for.

The fall-through's cost is smaller than it first looks, but it is not free. Where
that `catch` returned after `min(timeout, 10000) + 300` ms, the call now spends the
preliminary wait plus one probe deadline plus a poll, so the decision lands ~55 s
after the wait started — on top of everything the spec already spent reaching it,
against the 60 s per-test timeout in `playwright.config.ts`. The scenario that
costs is a page that starves AFTER load, before the nav wait; a page starved from
the start never gets this far, failing earlier in `waitForLuxarReady`'s own wait
with its own message. On such a page Playwright usually expires the test first at
all nine sites: the three whose next statement is an unbounded `page.evaluate`
(directly in `keyboard-input-system.spec.ts`, through the spec-local
`getDimensionValue` twice in `dimension-animation.spec.ts`) hang there either way,
and even the six `spatial-index-accuracy.spec.ts` sites, whose next statement is
the bounded `getLuxarState`, have typically spent enough of the 60 s reaching the
wait that the ~55 s decision lands past it. So the 300 ms return it replaced mostly
bought the same hang one statement later, and the crafted message often never
prints. What the change reliably buys is therefore not a run that ends instead of
expiring, but ATTRIBUTION whenever the decision IS reached: the report prints under
this helper's own name, budget and counts rather than surfacing as the next
statement's failure.

`waitForWebGLError` is bounded rather than split, because it returns data instead
of a verdict: `getWebGLErrors` is a bare `page.evaluate`, so the first unanswered
read blew straight through the helper's 5 s budget to the whole test's. Each read
is now raced against the remaining budget, with the sentinel a Node-allocated
array compared by identity rather than by contents — an empty list is a
legitimate answer here, one a clean GL queue returns on nearly every poll — and no
FURTHER read is dispatched into a remainder shorter than one poll interval, since
`getWebGLErrors` DRAINS the GL queue in-page and an answer discarded for missing
the deadline takes the real errors it already cleared with it. A read that spent
the whole remainder is also no longer followed by one more `pollMs` sleep past the
deadline, so a 1000 ms budget no longer reports 1100 ms elapsed. The first read is
exempt from the sliver-skip, so any positive `timeout`, even one under `pollMs`,
still checks the queue once instead of returning `[]` while warning that nothing
was checked — that exemption is also the one case where the call can still outlast
its budget, since an early answer there is followed by a full `pollMs` before the
guard re-checks. A non-positive `timeout` gets zero reads, the loop guard failing
on entry, and the warn then says nothing was checked, which is true. That trade is inherent to
bounding a helper that returns data; removing it means bounding `getWebGLErrors`
itself, which has 21 direct call sites across 11 spec files and belongs with the
probe-bounding work in #1651. The helper does not throw on give-up — it warns
once, saying whether any read answered and how many errors the accumulated union
held, so an unsatisfied predicate cannot be read as "the page was healthy and
produced no such error" — but it is not throw-free: a read that rejects, or a page
closed mid-poll, still propagates. It has no call sites today, so that contract
change is forward-looking.

`src/tests/unit/tests/e2e-helpers-silent-waits.test.ts` covers all three helpers
against fake pages, with the per-test redness against the old code recorded in its
header: a page whose probes reject makes both loops throw within their own budget;
a page whose probes never answer at all makes them throw too, carrying the probe's
own starvation diagnostic and a measured elapsed longer than the budget; an
answered-but-unsettled page resolves with exactly one warning carrying the probe
diagnostic, the probe counts, and the preliminary failure when there was one,
while a stale early failure is cleared in EITHER loop without dropping out of the
counts; a `null` answer warns and
returns on both loops rather than being read as no answer; a budget the
preliminary wait consumes whole is still probed exactly once, and a non-positive
one neither hangs in that wait nor claims probes it never attempted; the WebGL
loop accumulates across polls, propagates a rejecting read, and holds its budget
to the millisecond; and the healthy cases stay silent, which is the assertion that
protects the existing call sites.
