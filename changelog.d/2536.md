#### Remote-control embedder API: `flyTo`, rendering and layer setters, `getViewerState`, `camera-changed`

A Luxar viewer can now be driven from an external program without the built-in
UI. `LuxarApp.flyTo(pose, { durationMs, easing })` transitions smoothly to a
camera pose captured with `getCameraPose()`, interpolating in the orbit
parameterisation (the focus target moves linearly while the viewing direction is
slerped and the distance log-interpolated) so the camera arcs around the scene
instead of cutting through it, and lands on the pose exactly. Any input on the
canvas or keyboard cancels the flight where it is — the visitor's own hand always
wins — and the promise reports whether the flight completed.

`setRenderingSettings(patch)` rides the exact path an authored `viewer_config`
takes at load, so anything a scene author can bake a controller can set live, with
the same validation and side-effects; `setLayer(path, patch)` and `getLayers()`
expose the Layers panel's per-layer appearance through the panel's own
state-manager and apply-engine route; `getViewerState()` bundles dataset, camera,
slice position, rendering and layers in one call; and a new `camera-changed`
event streams the pose as it moves. `docs/guides/specs/REMOTE_CONTROL_SPEC.md`
records the design and the follow-on phases (WebSocket hub in `luxar serve`,
kiosk permissions in `viewer_config`).

Scenes can also now author **story waypoints**: `ViewerConfig(waypoints=[Waypoint(
when={"story": 1, "time": (10, 20)}, camera=CameraConfig(target_node="cluster_7"),
duration_ms=2500)])` binds a camera pose to a position along the hidden
dimensions, using the same `visible_range` rule overlays use (exact within ±0.5,
inclusive ranges, first match wins). A hidden discrete "story" dimension thus
carries a whole guided tour — colours in the data, captions as dimension-aware
overlays, and now the camera — driven by the `[` / `]` keys, the slider or an
external controller calling `setDimensionValue`. The viewer snaps to the
matching waypoint at load and flies on a change of match; fields a waypoint
leaves out keep the live camera's value, so a waypoint can re-aim without
moving, and an optional `rendering` block applies snake_case `ViewerConfig`
overrides on arrival. Waypoints compose with the orbit turntable: while
auto-rotate is on, a story step keeps the live viewing direction and only moves
the point the camera spins around, so the spin never pauses (`flyTo` exposes
this as `keepOrientation`). `CameraConfig` gains an ortho `zoom`, since under an
orthographic projection zoom, not distance, is what frames tighter.

A new demo, `esm3_protein_stories`, is the reference scene for all of this: the
ESM C Swiss-Prot 3D UMAP with a hidden `story` dimension that flies through five
protein-family clusters — hemoglobin, photosystem II D1, Hsp70/DnaK, the viral
surface proteins, the prion protein — each highlighted in its own layer and
captioned with a panel of sourced facts closing on an open question, and wrapped
in a faint translucent marker shell built from the existing mesh model. It reads
the landscape demo's cache and opens with the turntable on. A companion design
note, `docs/guides/specs/MESH_PHYSICAL_MATERIALS_SPEC.md`, records what that
mesh model cannot express (refraction, reflection, dispersion) and a phased plan
for physical materials on Luxar meshes.
