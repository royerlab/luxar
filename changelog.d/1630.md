#### One intensity scale for every tile of a tiled fit

A tiled fit normalized each tile by its **own** min/max, so every tile was
stretched to fill `[0, 1]` by a different factor. Output amplitudes are rescaled
by that same factor, so the physical amplitude of a linear fit largely cancels —
but everything the optimiser states as an absolute quantity in the normalized
range does not: the convergence tolerance (`max_abs_error`, 1% of it by
default), the seeding and culling thresholds, `amp_max`. A dim tile was
therefore resolved to a far finer physical accuracy than a bright one, and
neighbouring tiles' splats were not mutually comparable.

`resolve_volume_norm_range` mirrors `resolve_volume_floor`: same bounded
sampler, same 32M-voxel budget, same determinism guarantee, so every worker
fitting one array — `--tile k/M`, `-j N`, or a uniform-tiled `batch-fit` task —
resolves the same range without coordinating. `fit_tiled` resolves it once and
hands it to every tile; `fit_tile` resolves it itself when called standalone.
A `batch-fit` task's array is one `(t, c)`, so the range is shared across that
timepoint's tiles rather than across the whole timelapse — unlike the floor,
which the planner pins globally.

Three measurements carry no usable scale, and each makes the tiles fall back to
their own with a printed note rather than share it: a non-finite top; a top with
no positive extent, where the bounded sample landed entirely in empty or masked
space (sharing a zero range fills every tile with a uniform 0.5 and fits a
fabricated flat field; a negative one inverts the sign); and a top the applied
floor reaches, which leaves nothing but the epsilon the shift manufactures. That
last one is reachable through the single-tile worker, which applies a numeric
`--floor` unguarded on purpose, and normalizing by that epsilon would have made
real signal ~1e12 times too dark. A volume that is honestly dim is none of these
and keeps its shared scale.

`image_min` is pinned at zero for tiles — that is where the array a tile fitter
sees starts, since the floor subtraction clips at 0 and the Hann window tapers
every overlapped face to 0. A positive `image_min` would subtract a constant
from both sides of an overlap and clip the taper away, re-introducing the seam.
A supplied full-range scale carries no ceiling either: it is estimated from a
bounded sample, so a voxel above it is real signal rather than an outlier to
clip. A `norm_percentile > 0` range asked for outlier clipping and keeps its
ceiling. The auto `amp_max` follows that peak rather than capping at 1.0: the
cap is a `clamp` with no gradient above it, so a hard 1.0 would pin the
brightest splats and flatten the very peak the unclipped scale exists to
preserve — it stays exactly 1.0 whenever the range came from the array itself.
A supplied range must be finite with `image_max > image_min`; a reversed or
empty one used to fit "successfully" on nonsense (an empty range lands in the
near-uniform branch, which replaces the array with 0.5). Residual passes of a
progressive fit keep their own per-pass scale; only pass 0 shares the
whole-volume one.

Whole-volume fits are unaffected. One behaviour change worth knowing for tiled
fits: a tile far dimmer than the volume maximum is now held to the same
*absolute* tolerance as the rest of the volume, so it converges earlier instead
of resolving its own noise at full contrast — which is the whole-volume fit's
behaviour a tiled fit is meant to approximate. `--tiling content` boxes still
normalize against their own crop (see #1616).
