#### A fit now records how well it reconstructed the signal, not just the volume

Global PSNR is a poor summary of a sparse fit. On a light-sheet stack that is
99.9% empty, a reconstruction that discards nine tenths of the signal still
scores 37 dB, because most of what it got right was the emptiness. Every fit
now also records `foreground_psnr_db` — the same PSNR, averaged over
`target > otsu(target)` instead of over everything — which scores that same
reconstruction at 0.9 dB.

Alongside it go `foreground_threshold` and `foreground_fraction`, because a dB
figure taken over 0.01% of a volume means something quite different from one
taken over 40%, and a reader cannot tell which they are looking at otherwise.
`gsplat info` and `gsplat compare` print all three; the trio lands in
`fitting/` beside the global figure.

Foreground is defined on the *target*, so a fit that hallucinates structure is
still scored where the signal actually is, and `data_range` is taken from the
whole volume rather than from the foreground's own narrower span — matching
`held_out_psnr_foreground`, and avoiding an inflation that would otherwise be
invisible.

Otsu's threshold is computed in `luxar.gsplats.metrics` rather than delegated
to scikit-image. That library lives in the `demos` extra, and the existing
calibration helper quietly falls back to `V.min()` when the import fails —
tolerable for a seeding heuristic, not for a number that gets published, since
the *definition* of foreground would then depend on which extras happened to be
installed. The reimplementation is pinned against `skimage.filters.threshold_otsu`
to the exact histogram bin.
