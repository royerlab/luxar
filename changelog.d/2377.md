#### Dimension playback no longer renders only the coarsest LOD level (#2377)

A progressive loader running under a playback frame budget committed LOD 0 and
nothing else. That assumes LOD 0 is a usable picture, and on a node the viewer
*slices* it need not be: an additive ladder's rungs are sized against the whole
node, so a 500-timepoint gsplat leaf puts a median of 45 splats per timepoint in
LOD 0. Measured against the deployed viewer, playing the time dimension of
`gsplats_4d_drosophila_embryogenesis` at the default 10 fps rendered 20-51 of the
166,443 splats in a frame — a visually empty embryo — while still pulling 3-5
MB/s off the network. At 1 fps it was perfect. Two other shipped demos sit on the
same curve: playback renders *exactly* the predicted LOD-0 count in each case
(nexrad_supercell 244, neuromast_2ch 14,243), while every demo on a four-level
ladder gets 25% of its frame and looks fine.

The old gate bundled two rules into one expression. The first is kept: a pass
that restored a cached prefix commits it as-is, since the prefix already shows
something and deepening it is the background prefetch's job. The second is
dropped: a pass that starts empty now streams levels that come back
cache-resident until its budget is spent, stopping at the first cold or slow one
exactly as a `refine` pass does.

Residency, not level index, is what separates affordable-inside-a-tick from
stalls-the-tick — a cold level costs hundreds of milliseconds against a 60 ms
budget at 10 fps, a resident one 10-20 ms. So the worst case is unchanged at one
cold load per pass, which is what the floor-only behaviour already cost, and
there is no new network at all: the coarse rungs of a sliced ladder are small
enough to sit in one chunk each, so they are fetched once and serve every slice.
On drosophila the viewer was refusing 9,843 splats per timepoint that it had
already downloaded.

The authoring half of the problem — a first rung sized against the node rather
than the slice, which affects eight demos across two corpora — is separate and
tracked in #2376.
