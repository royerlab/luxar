#### A downscaled parallel tiled fit writes split planes in the splats' own frame

`luxar gsplat fit --tiling uniform --downscale N -j>1` wrote a `kind=partition`
whose `bsp_tree` split planes were stated in DOWNSCALED voxels while the splats
inside the parts were in full-resolution ones. Every plane was therefore a
factor of N too small and no longer lay between the parts it separated, so the
viewer's back-to-front part ordering (#1555) was computed against planes that
separate nothing — the seam popping that tree exists to prevent, back again on
exactly the datasets large enough to need `--downscale`. Part LABELS were always
correct, and a fit without `--downscale` was never affected.

The frame mismatch is deliberate, not accidental: `dispatch_parallel_tiled`
computes its tile grid on the post-downscale shape so that the parent and its
`fit --tile i/M` workers agree on the tile count M, while each worker rescales
its own splats back to original coordinates before writing. What was missing was
the reconciliation at the merge. `grid_bsp_tree` now takes an optional per-axis
`scale`, and the resolved downscale factors are threaded from the dispatcher
through `fit_tiled_parallel` and `merge_tile_results` to that one call site — a
tile spanning `[origin, origin + shape)` maps to
`[origin * f, (origin + shape) * f)`, the same convention `rescale_centers`
applies to the centers. Nothing else consumes the factors; `volume_shape` keeps
its existing meaning (the shape the grid was computed on) for the stats block
and the empty-result fallback.

No other producer of these planes can be in a downscaled frame: the sequential
tiled path writes a flat leaf under `--downscale` (no tree at all), `--tiling
content` declares `--downscale` unsupported and ignores it, and the `batch-fit`
merge has no downscale option anywhere in its pipeline.
