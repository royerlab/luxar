#### The demo manifest pins the archives that actually exist

Every Zenodo-hosted demo archive has been refitted, re-encoded, or rebuilt since
the manifest last recorded it, so all 32 `bytes`/`sha256` pins named files that
no longer exist anywhere. A demo would have downloaded the current archive and
then failed its own integrity check.

Each new pin was derived the other way round — from what the deposition holds.
Zenodo reports an MD5 per file; every pin here comes from a local copy whose MD5
matches, and the recorded `sha256` is that copy's. Nothing is transcribed from a
build log.

One pin is the exception, and it is deliberate. `zebrafish_4d.gsplats.zarr.zip`
was rebuilt with component filtering after it was uploaded, so its pin describes
the current build rather than the deposition's; the draft has to be replaced
before publication. The blastocyst pair was renamed after upload, so the drafts
hold the pinned bytes under the former `organoids_ch*` names and need renaming
too. `scripts/zenodo_prepublish_check.py` fails on all three until that is done.

The set is now uniformly zarr format 3. That was already true of the seventeen
archives the refit wrote directly and of the 546 per-timepoint stores inside the
three timelapse bundles; what remained was the DESI scene, which the rebuild
converts, and the nine archives re-encoded in place with `gsplat reencode -e auto`.

The superseded in-repo LFS archives are removed so the manifest generator no
longer overwrites these hosted pins from stale local pointers. The three assets a
demo also reads straight off disk — `census_umap_1m.npz` and the two
`3d_umap_coords_*.parquet` files — remain in the repository, and theirs are the
only `zenodo` pins still describing a shipped copy. (`dipc_gm12878.npz` stays
too, but it is `regenerate`, not hosted.) Until all three Zenodo
records are published and their manifest URLs enabled, the hosted archives are
not fetchable; this change must remain draft until that publication step lands.
