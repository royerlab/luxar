#### A tiled fit scores the archive it actually ships

Every tile of a tiled fit scores itself, but the tiles overlap and are
Hann-apodized, so their errors do not compose into the merged one — and the
merged result is what gets written. A tiled archive therefore carried no
`psnr_db` and no `foreground_psnr_db` at all, which is precisely what a
published dataset is asked to state.

`fit_tiled` now renders the merged reconstruction once against the whole volume
it still has in hand and stamps the metrics. When the fit returned physical
coordinates the merged splats are first mapped back onto the tile grid: the two
frames differ by one per-axis factor that scales centers directly and Cholesky
row `i` by `scale[i]`, so dividing both inverts it exactly. That inverse is the
part worth testing, and it is: the same fit scored with and without the
conversion must agree, which a mutation collapses from 46 dB to 25 dB.

Scoring is capped (24 GiB by default, `LUXAR_TILED_QUALITY_MAX_GB` to raise it)
because it runs *before* the archive is written, so an over-budget score does not
cost a metric — it costs the whole fit. The cap is measured against the real
peak, which sits inside SSIM rather than at the render: the reconstruction, the
reference, and the six convolution intermediates SSIM keeps live, whose own
tiled fallback engages on CUDA only. When the cap declines, or the score fails,
it says so out loud even under `--quiet` — an archive that silently carries no
PSNR is the failure this exists to end. Two cases are deliberately left unscored
and say why, printing under `--quiet` for the same reason: a `kind=partition`
result, which has nowhere to persist fit stats, and the parallel worker path,
which is handed only the tile grid's shape and not the volume.
`luxar gsplat compare` remains the recourse for both, preceded by
`luxar gsplat flatten` when the result is a partition — `compare` loads a
matrix-shaped store and cannot read one otherwise.
