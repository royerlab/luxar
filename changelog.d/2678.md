#### Hosted gsplat loads tolerate multiplexed slow links again (#2678)

The response-body watchdog now follows aggregate fetch progress instead of
aborting each quiet HTTP/2 stream independently. Multiplexing queue time before
a stream's first byte no longer consumes its absolute body deadline or retry
budget, while dead connections and true trickles remain bounded.

Chunk-body concurrency is reduced from 64 to 24, and zarr metadata plus cache
validation use a separate four-request lane. Small `zarr.json` and `.zattrs`
probes therefore cannot be trapped behind a full wave of large chunk bodies.
