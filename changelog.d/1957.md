#### Rebuild a demo scene when its builder changes, and warn before a node overflows its element texture

`ocean_currents_earth` was reported with a hard-edged wedge of missing
streamlines over the North Atlantic. The data was never missing: the scene on
disk had been built before the ribbons were partitioned, so all 11,440,000
segments sat in one Lines node — above the 11,173,888 a single node can hold on
a 16384-class GPU. The viewer clamped the tail, and because segments are stored
in Hilbert order the lost 2.3% was one contiguous spatial lobe rather than a
scatter, which is why it read as a hole in the ocean instead of a capacity
limit. The partitioning fix had landed three weeks earlier; nothing rebuilt the
stale store, because a demo reused its output whenever the path merely existed.

Four source-built demo scenes now include a builder fingerprint — a hash of
that demo module's source — in either the scene root or their existing build
marker, and rebuild when it no longer matches. `--keep-stale` remains an escape
hatch when a source-only mismatch may not justify an expensive reassembly. The
DESI galaxies demo is the exception: it may unpack a shipped prebuilt scene
with no local builder fingerprint, so it retains its targeted stale-ladder
diagnostic rather than forcing a ~1 GB download and rebuild on every run. The
fingerprint check gates scene assembly only: downloads, gsplat fits and
precomputed bundles keep their own caches, so an edit costs a scene rebuild and
never a re-fetch or a re-fit.

The compiler now also warns at authoring time when a node exceeds what one
element texture can render on a 4096-class GPU — 2,793,472 segments, 5,591,040
points or 4,194,304 splats — including the concatenated total of an additive
ladder. The warning names `partition=dict(max_elements=…)` for scene-authored
geometry and also points standalone GSplat datasets to
`luxar gsplat lod --recipe tiles`. The caps live in
`luxar.typing_utils.constants` alongside the derivation that mirrors the viewer
layout.

`ocean_currents_earth` itself picks up the appearance it was meant to have: the
globe renders `opaque` (the only mode that leaves the viewer's sorted
transparent set and unconditionally depth-writes, so it is the only one that
reliably composites *under* the ribbons in front of it) and the currents sit at
opacity 0.77. Its opening camera now targets the origin — the centre of the
Earth — instead of a point just below the near surface, so the globe starts
centred and the first drag orbits the planet about its axis rather than swinging
it about a surface point.
