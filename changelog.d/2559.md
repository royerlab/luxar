#### Mesh physical materials, Phases 1 and 2: `material="physical"`, glass, live knobs

A mesh can now opt out of the house shader's light-free, view-anchored key and
into three.js's physically based material — `MeshPhysicalMaterial` on WebGL,
`MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial` on WebGPU — with `add_mesh(..., material="physical")`
and the knobs `roughness`, `metalness`, `clearcoat`, `clearcoat_roughness`,
`iridescence`, `sheen` (each in `[0, 1]`) and `sheen_color`. That unlocks what the
house model could never express: polished and brushed metals, a pearlescent
shell, velvet, and a true view-relative Fresnel rim from `clearcoat` on a dark
translucent base — the marker-shell look the protein-stories demo wanted. The
viewer lights these from a scene environment (a prefiltered `RoomEnvironment`)
that is built lazily on the first physical mesh and never otherwise, so every
existing scene keeps `scene.environment` empty and renders exactly as before;
house materials do not read it in any case.

Phase 2 adds the glass family: `transmission` (`[0, 1]`), `ior` (`[1, 2.333]`),
`thickness`, `attenuation_color` with `attenuation_distance`, and `dispersion`,
rendered by three's stock transmission pass on both backends. Glass refracts the
background and other meshes — not Luxar's points, lines or splats, which three's
pass never sees and which stay crisp on top, in front of or behind the glass, on
both backends (a transmissive mesh is ordered first within its `layer_order` band
so WebGPU, where glass shares the transparent list with the emissive layers,
cannot paint over a cluster that sorts behind it). The Layers panel's physical
knob listing became live sliders: dragging `Transmission` on a metal turns it to
glass, an `Attenuation distance` slider's top stop means "none", and Reset
restores what `add_mesh(...)` authored.

Authoring is fail-loud: a physical knob without `material="physical"` is refused,
a physical mesh refuses the house-shader knobs, `blending_mode`, a colormap, a
texture and `shading="none"`, and `thickness` / `attenuation_*` / `dispersion`
are refused without a `transmission` above zero — none of these means anything in
those pairings. `shading` smooth/flat and `alpha_cutoff` are kept — the first is
a property of any lit surface, the second maps onto three's `alphaTest`.
Translucency is read off the data (an `opacity` below 1, a `transmission` above
0, or per-vertex alpha without a cutoff); a physical mesh is never
triangle-sorted but keeps its `layer_order` band, picks through the house
mesh-pick material, and works with partitions, LOD groups and nD slicing as any
mesh does.

Ships with `demo_mesh_physical_materials` (nine spheres — house shader, clearcoat
shell, gold, steel, pearl, velvet, clear glass, amber glass, dispersive crystal —
in front of a checkerboard for the glass to bend, with emissive clusters inside
the shell and the clear glass), a public `luxar.mesh.primitives.icosphere()`, and
a real-WebGPU-versus-WebGL screenshot A/B script
(`scripts/ab-webgpu-vs-webgl.mjs`, judged by structural similarity).
