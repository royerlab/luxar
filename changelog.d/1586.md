#### A whole *Drosophila* brain as Gaussian splats (FlyLight MCFO, 63x)

A new gsplat demo, `gsplats_3d_flylight_mcfo_63x_brain`: a female central brain
plus both optic lobes, MultiColor FlpOut-labelled, as 653,759 splats against a
2573 x 2707 x 463 confocal stack of which only 0.014% rises above 1% of the peak
intensity — roughly 4,900:1. This is the shape splats are for; the empty 99.99%
costs nothing.

MCFO is a stochastic three-epitope label, so a neuron's hue is the *ratio* of
three channels at the same voxels. The fit therefore runs once, on the
gain-balanced per-voxel maximum, and each splat samples its colour at its own
centre — a near-even 41 / 27 / 31% three-way split here, with 0.06% of splats
uncoloured. Three independent per-channel fits would not co-locate and every
axon would render candy-striped. The nc82 neuropil reference channel is
excluded: at 10-19% occupancy it is not sparse and would dominate the fit.

The source is Janelia's stitched 8-bit H5J. Assembling the ten raw 16-bit LSM
tiles ourselves was tried and abandoned. The raw tiles carry no stage
coordinates — `Positions`, `TilePositions` and `OriginX/Y/Z` are all zero — and
once placement is recovered by registration, residual misalignment *varies* by
4.2-7.5 voxels across a single overlap, so no per-tile translation can align
them; Janelia's pipeline advertises "stitched **and** distortion corrected" for
exactly that reason. Blending N overlapping tiles also averages N independent
noise realisations, so doubly-covered regions come out quieter by sqrt(2)
(measured 1.442 against 1.414 predicted) along rectangular coverage boundaries,
which the fit sees as box-shaped steps. The 8-bit product costs dynamic range in
the dim tail and nothing in resolution: the raw tiles union to 462 x 2712 x 2577
against the H5J's 463 x 2707 x 2573 — the same grid to within 0.2%, at the same
0.19 um sampling.

The demo ships the appearance it was tuned at (volumetric blending, absorption
0.81, a 0-2.723 display window), and the splats are levelled by their own
amplitude-weighted principal axis so the brain sits straight and fills the canvas
rather than lying diagonally across a square mosaic footprint.

It also bakes an explicit camera, because the viewer's default frames a cube of
the box's *largest* dimension plus 20% margin, which leaves a 663 x 303 brain
small. The fit is taken at the object's near face rather than its centre plane —
the frustum narrows towards the camera, so a centre-plane fit puts the near
corners outside the frame (7% of the width, here). Since `fov` is vertical and
this brain is 2.19:1, width binds at every realistic aspect and no single baked
distance can fill it everywhere: the declared `CAMERA_ASPECT` is the landscape
aspect the framing is calibrated for, wider windows leave margin, narrower ones
crop, and framing for a square window instead would land within a few percent of
the viewer's own default — which is what the explicit camera exists to improve on.

`CLAUDE.md` and the `luxar-gsplat-pipeline` skill now say plainly what this
dataset taught: **stay on `--floor auto`**. A `pN` floor subtracts a percentile
of *all* voxels, so on sparse data it lands wherever the sparsity puts it rather
than where the noise ends. On the crop the floor sweep was run over, `p99` sat
at 1.34% of that crop's max — inside real signal. (Whole-volume percentiles are
not the same number: over the full stack p99 is 0.05% of peak. A percentile
floor is exactly this unanchored, which is the point.) At a fixed seed budget,
scored against the unfloored original, `auto` recovers
40.7% of the dim band (1-10% of max, where thin faint neurites live) and comes
within 0.25 dB of no floor at all; `p99` recovers 0.6%, loses 9.4 dB of
foreground PSNR, and yields a third of the splats from the same seeds. The trap
is that a high floor *looks* better in a MIP — the haze is gone and the render
is crisper than its own source. Judge a floor on foreground and dim-band PSNR
against unfloored data, never on how the render looks.
