#### FlyWire connectome: luminous neurons, ACES tone mapping, and the exception retired (#1459)

`demo_flywire_connectome` now bakes the hand-tuned neuron appearance — every
`Neurons — …` layer at `opacity=0.79` with `blending_mode="luminous"` (was 0.95 and
the default `additive`) — and moves the scene from `Neutral` to the house `ACES`.
That retires the last non-gsplat `Neutral` exception, whose stated condition was an
A/B render, and this is it. ACES costs about half a stop on this scene: captured
through the gallery harness at the shipped -1.0 stops the still goes from 1.9% of its
lit pixels blown (`Neutral`) to 5.4%, just past the harness's own 5% clip guard. So
the demo's gallery exposure override moves to -1.5 stops, where the still is back to
0.9% blown with a 0.50 histogram spread — the `Neutral` still's profile — and keeps
its structure and its super-class palette.

The connections keep `opacity=0.08`. #1459 asked for 0.79 on those layers too, to
match the neurons, and the render is what refused it: 300 000 luminous segments at
0.79 blow 78% of the frame's lit pixels to white and the fly brain becomes a
featureless blob — exactly the wash the old comment described. It is not an exposure
problem: given 2.5 extra stops of headroom (the harness's own auto-exposure lands at
-3.45 rather than -0.91) 17% is still blown and the blue optic lobes, orange central
brain and green sensory clusters have all gone achromatic; even 0.24 pales the optic
lobes. A brighter connective glow needs fewer edges or a per-layer intensity
rebalance, not a slider.
