#### Partitions carry, keep, and transform their split planes

A `kind=partition` gsplat node can record the split planes of the decomposition that produced its parts (`bsp_tree`), and the viewer traverses them to order parts back-to-front exactly — correct for any camera pose, including inside the volume. Without that record it falls back to sorting parts by their content centroid, which is not a valid painter's order: it flips discretely as the camera moves, so an order-dependent blending mode (`normal` or `volumetric`) pops at the seams between parts on every orbit.

Three separate defects meant that record was usually missing. **It was never produced by a tiled fit:** the content planner has always *been* a recursive BSP, but it discarded its own split planes and kept only the flat box list, so `fit --tiling content|uniform` and every `batch-fit` merge emitted a partition that could not say how its parts stacked up. **It was silently dropped on rebuild:** `GSplatPartition.bsp_tree` defaults to `None` and both generic tree-rebuild helpers omitted the field, so a `tiles`/`adaptive` dataset lost exact ordering the moment it passed through `gsplat transform` — including for intensity-only transforms that move no centers at all — or through `gsplat additive`, whose documented contract is that partition parts keep their shape. **And nothing mapped it when centers moved:** `split` is a coordinate in the centers' own space, so preserving a tree verbatim across a geometry transform would have been worse than dropping it, since a stale plane still yields a plausible-looking part order and the ordering would degrade with no sign that anything was wrong.

All three are fixed. The planner keeps its recursion and hands it to the fitted partition; the tiled, content-tiled and batch-merge paths all carry it through; and `gsplat transform` now maps the planes through the same affine it applies to the centers — translations, per-axis scales and quarter-turn rotations map exactly (a reflection also swaps each node's halves), while an arbitrary rotation shears the cells out of axis-alignment, which the format cannot express, so that tree is dropped and the ordering downgrade is announced rather than left to be discovered in the viewer.

The subtle part throughout is numbering. Every tiled producer plans more regions than it writes — a box whose budget rounds to zero, a tile that fits no splats, a part emptied by a cull, a batch slot empty at every timepoint — and parts are numbered over the *survivors*. A tree still labelled with pre-drop indices would point at the wrong parts while remaining a perfectly valid permutation, so a single shared helper prunes and renumbers leaves against the surviving set, and a tree that cannot be reconciled with the parts is discarded rather than half-applied.

Uniform tiling is the one approximate case, and is documented as such: its apodized tiles keep their overlap band, so neighbouring parts genuinely share space and no exact order exists. The cuts are placed at the midplane of each shared band, which confines any misordering to that band instead of letting whole tiles swap.

On the viewer side, the within-group comparator no longer mixes BSP ranks with centroid depth. That mix is non-transitive whenever a stored tree names only some of a wrapper's parts, and `Array.sort` may then return an order satisfying neither key; the group now falls back to depth wholesale, which is approximate but well defined.
