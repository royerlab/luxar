#### Stop asserting SSIM agreement to five decimal places by accident

`test_partition_metrics_match_the_same_flat_splats` compared eight metrics with a
single `pytest.approx(..., rel=1e-3)`. A relative bound cannot constrain a metric
that sits near zero, and SSIM here is -0.0114 — the reference volume and its splat
rendering are essentially uncorrelated at this size — so the budget collapsed to
1.14e-05. The measured flat-vs-partition difference is 1.154e-05: an overshoot of
1.011x, budget and error agreeing to within one percent.

That is float summation order, which is stable on a given platform and differs
across them, so the test was deterministically red on some machines and green on
others. It is not flaky, and the equality claim in its name holds — partition
metrics do equal flat metrics to 1.15e-05 on a metric bounded by 1. Nothing about
the partitioned compare path is wrong.

Adding `abs=1e-4` gives the near-zero metrics a floor while leaving `rel` binding
for everything of order 1, since `pytest.approx` passes on either bound. Raising
`rel` would have been the wrong fix: it weakens `psnr` and `max_abs_error`, which
are the metrics actually carrying the claim.
