#### The demos' `Neutral` tone-mapping pins are now chosen by range

Seven files in the demo package set `tone_mapping="Neutral"` on a
`ViewerConfig`, most with some version of the same comment: colour here is an
exact encoding, and ACES shifts hues. (Two argued differently —
`demo_flywire_connectome` said ACES "blew the 300K-line luminous connection glow
out into a white wash", and `_interop_common` said the imported RGB is already
display-referred.) Five demos are swept here; the shared
`_interop_common` helper is treated separately below, and
`demo_flywire_connectome`'s own flip to a re-tuned ACES is PR #1527's half of
#1459, so this sweep only records its row in the new guard's exception table.
The premise is right; the conclusion only follows over range. `Neutral`
(Khronos PBR Neutral) is not a passthrough anywhere — it subtracts an offset
(0.04 once a channel minimum reaches 0.08, `x - 6.25*x*x` below that, which
crushes near-black hardest) and then, from peak 0.76 up, compresses the peak and
mixes toward the achromatic value *equal to that compressed peak*, with weight
`g = 1 - 1/(0.15*(peak - newPeak) + 1)`. Running the three.js formula:
`(1, 0, 0)` leaves it at `(0.880, 0.016, 0.016)`, `(0.5, 0.5, 0.5)` — well
inside gamut — at `(0.46, 0.46, 0.46)`, `(8, 0.5, 8)` at
`(0.992, 0.535, 0.992)` (g = 0.51) and `(100, 0, 0)` at
`(0.999, 0.936, 0.936)` (g = 0.94), very nearly white. For a scene whose colours
already fit inside [0, 1], the pin was dulling exactly the encoding its comment
set out to protect.

Over range, what Neutral costs is **chroma, not hue**. Its compress-and-mix is
"scale every channel, then add the same amount to all", which preserves the HSV
hue angle exactly: `(100, 0, 0)` stays at hue 0° and drops to saturation 0.06.
A hard clamp is the mirror image — `(100, 0, 0)` clamps to `(1, 0, 0)`, full
saturation, but `(2, 1, 0)` clamps to `(1, 1, 0)` and moves from hue 30° to hue
60°, and every structure above 1.0 goes flat; ACES shifts hue by design. So
nothing is faithful over range, and the choice is which distortion you can live
with — where colour is a hue encoding, Neutral is the one that keeps the hue.

`"None"` is the exact choice inside [0, 1]: the viewer maps it to
`THREE.NoToneMapping`, which the mega-shader aliases to its Linear (clamp) mode,
and exposure, offset and gamma still apply because they run *before* the
tone-mapping switch. Two demos moved there on that reasoning —
`demo_ocean_currents_earth` (Blue Marble texture plus a blue→white speed LUT,
both in [0, 1], normal blending at intensity 1.0) and
`demo_gsplats_recipes_tribolium` (an hsv-derived hue/shade palette over six
volumetric gsplat nodes, one per `RECIPES` entry, each of whose emitted radiance
works out to `color * (1 - exp(-tau)) / absorption` with `tau = absorption *
opacity * intensity` — `opacity` cancels identically, so the per-node in-gamut
bound comes from `absorption=1.0`; their `One`/`OneMinusSrcAlpha` composition of
bounded premultiplied sources keeps the sum bounded too, and the demo's
`add_text` labels are DOM overlays outside the HDR pass entirely. A viewer who
drags the Layers panel's live κ slider below 1 can push a node over range
again).

The remaining three keep `Neutral`, and their comments now say why rather than
merely that they always have. All three run over range, and for the first two
colour is a hue encoding — `demo_nd_transforms` (a deliberately HDR lit palette
running to 2.4, where a red R must read as red) and
`demo_biodiversity_planetary_scale` (`GLOBE_INTENSITY` 4.88,
`OCCURRENCE_INTENSITY` 100.0 over a categorical taxonomic palette) — so Neutral
is exactly the right operator there: it is the hue-exact one over range, and it
sheds the chroma of the brightest peaks to get that. `demo_gsplats_3d_tribolium_embryo`
(`exposure=1.97`, a peak-projecting blending mode) keeps its own reason: the pin
was verified against that volume, and Neutral rolls its peaks off instead of
clipping them flat. Re-pointing any of them at ACES or at `"None"` trades hue
fidelity for chroma, so it needs a live A/B render rather than a blind flip. The
shared `_interop_common.build_interop_scene`
default keeps `"Neutral"` for a narrower reason: the imported captures carry
per-splat RGB clipped to [0, 1], but `demo_gsplats_interop_sog_matrixcity` builds
`recipe="overview"`, whose substitutive merge emits amplitudes with no bound of
1 that the gsplat `normal` shader feeds straight into RGB — so that one needs a
render check before it can claim to be in gamut.

A new AST guard, `demos/tests/test_demos_tone_mapping_policy.py`, pins the
policy: every statically known `tone_mapping` in the demo package — literal,
signature default, or module-level string constant — must be `"ACES"` unless its
module carries a row (with a reason) in the test's exception table. The house
rule in `CLAUDE.md` and `HDR_GUIDE.md`, `gsplat convert`'s own `--help` and
docstring, the compiler's LUT notice, `ViewerConfig.tone_mapping` and the
viewer's tone-mapping tooltip were all saying "reach for Neutral when you need
exact colour"; they now say to pick by range — `None` inside [0, 1], and over
range come back into range, accept ACES's rolloff, or keep Neutral where hue
matters more than the chroma of the peaks.
