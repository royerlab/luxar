#### Guard the manifest attribution against the published record text

A dataset's provenance is stated twice: in `data_manifest.json`'s `attribution`
field, and in the record description published on Zenodo and captured under
`scripts/zenodo_record_text/`. Nothing compared the two, and they had already
drifted. The manifest says of the Drosophila timelapse that "the imaging itself
is unpublished; for the SiMView instrument see Royer et al., Nat. Biotechnol.
34, 1267-1278 (2016)", while the live record says "Imaging described in Royer et
al., ..." and, at record level, "The imaging is described in ... Users of this
record should cite that publication in addition to this record." That reads as a
data publication. The imaging was never separately deposited or published; the
2016 paper describes the instrument it was acquired with. The drift was caught
by eye, once, which is not a mechanism.

`scripts/check_record_attribution.py` is now that mechanism. For every hosted
dataset whose imaging is said to be unpublished, it locates that dataset's
bullet in the captured record text and reports any claim that a publication
describes, documents or publishes *the imaging*. The subject is the whole
question, in both directions. A publication describing the *instrument* or the
*method* is the correct framing, so "the imaging instrument is described in
Royer et al.", "Imaging method: Royer, ...", "Imaging was carried out as
described in Royer et al.", "Imaging was performed as previously described in
Royer et al." and "published as a single 4D archive" all stay clean, as does any
denial ("the imaging has not been published in any article"); a claim is only
raised when the imaging itself is what the publication covers, within one
sentence of it. The guards are kept narrow on purpose, because a guard that
reaches past its purpose is pure false-negative surface: only a replacement
*head* noun clears a claim (an artefact noun in that gap is usually a modifier
of the imaging, as in "the imaging in this archive is described in ..."), and
only "not" and "never" read as a denial ("with no post-processing" does not).
Block-closing tags terminate a sentence, so a `<h3>` heading or a `<td>` cell —
both live in these records — cannot merge into the paragraph after it and
manufacture a claim neither block makes. Quotes keep the citation whole rather
than stopping at the period in "et al." or "Nat. Biotechnol.", because the
reference is what makes the report actionable. The recall bounds are documented
rather than implied: a gap longer than 80 characters, or crossing a full stop or
a semicolon, is not seen.

Either side may raise the unpublished claim — the manifest attribution or the
record's own bullet — so rewording one of them cannot quietly drop a dataset out
of the audit, and the inverse drift (a record claiming unpublished imaging while
the manifest cites a publication) is examined too. Record-level prose names no
dataset: it is attributed to one only when every dataset on that record makes
the same claim, and is otherwise reported as a record-scoped `[HUMAN]` line
naming the record and quoting the sentence, for a person to attribute. `make
check-record-attribution` runs it, and it joins `make check-external-references`
as the first leg of that aggregator needing no network. That runner grades this
leg by exit status, not by its markers, so the command exits non-zero for a
human check as well as for drift; only a missing or unreadable input grades
ERROR.

Scope is resolved before any `[CONFIG]`, so adding a manifest entry and
capturing its record later — an ordinary sequence — is silent rather than a
broken input that would escalate the whole leg to ERROR and relabel real drift
elsewhere as "could not run". A `[CONFIG]` means an input that was genuinely
needed is missing or unreadable, or that nothing at all was in scope.

It is deliberately report-only rather than a test, and that extends to the
tests. The live record text is authored on Zenodo by the maintainer, so the
finding above stands in this repo until that edit lands and
`scripts/zenodo_record_text/capture.py` re-captures it; a hard gate would just
be red. `scripts/tests` runs inside the required `python-tests` job, so the
one test that exercises the live files asserts only properties this repository
controls — that the audit runs, reports documented levels, and blames nothing
that is not a manifest dataset. It does not assert a count, a level or the
absence of a finding, because a single sentence added on Zenodo raises a
`[HUMAN]` line, and a test asserting otherwise would redden a required check on
the very re-capture PR that fixes the drift. Its docstring says so. The failure
message names the remedy, because the obvious wrong fix — regenerating the
snapshot from `gen_zenodo_records.py` — would destroy hand-maintained prose the
generator never produced.
