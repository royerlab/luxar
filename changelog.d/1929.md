#### The lift-parity spec now brackets the peak-mode divergence instead of noting it

Points and their lifted-gsplat twin agree on the ray mass in every sum mode, but
peak projection (`max`, `normal`, `opaque`) is still uncalibrated: the lift bakes
one amplitude for both shader branches and only the sum branch applies the ray
integral. The parity spec asserted that gap with a one-sided `> 1.1` on the crop
MEAN — a bar that sat inside the 10% parity band's own width, so it could not
tell a real 30× divergence from measurement noise, and a one-sided one at that.
The peak arm now reads the crop PEAK (the quantity peak projection actually
calibrates, and the one immune to `opaque`'s depth-driven coverage collapse) and
brackets it two ways: by DIRECTION per mode, and by a per-mode, per-radius
EXPECTED magnitude within a factor of 1.5 either way. An effect-C change now
goes red and its message names the value that moved.

Each cell also prints mean, peak and coverage side by side. A mean ratio alone
cannot say WHY it moved — under `opaque` a gsplat quad stamps one depth across
its whole footprint, so faint tails depth-reject the bright cores behind them and
coverage collapses on peaks that are bit-identical to `max`'s. The table is
emitted before the guards run, so a dead cell is diagnosed against the rows
measured either side of it rather than throwing mid-build and printing nothing.

Measuring the peak also surfaced the independent point/line `opaque` blending
defect fixed by #1994. With alpha-over restored, the points peak grows with
radius while the gsplat peak retains effect C, so the ratio crosses parity and
the lifted twin becomes increasingly dimmer toward the coarse radii. The opaque
arm therefore asserts the measured r=0.15 and r=0.40 cells rather than the two
fine cells used by `max` and `normal`.
