#### `luxar gsplat doctor`: diagnose an existing dataset, and repair what can be repaired

Some problems with a `.gsplats.zarr` are invisible. The store loads, the scene renders, and nothing errors — it is simply missing something a later Luxar learned to record, or carrying metadata that went stale under an edit. `gsplat doctor` is where that class of condition gets named, costed and, where the correct value can be recovered from the store itself, fixed in place without re-fitting.

It reports by default and only writes when asked. `luxar gsplat doctor data.gsplats.zarr` prints the full `info` report followed by a diagnosis and exits non-zero while a problem is still standing, so it can gate a pipeline; `--fix` applies the repairs; `--json` writes the findings out for a machine. Repairs are metadata-level and share one finalize, which re-stamps the root `content_hash` and re-consolidates metadata — the consolidated copy *shadows* the per-node attrs a repair writes, so skipping that would leave every fix invisible to readers while looking applied on disk.

The first condition it knows about is a partition with no split planes (`bsp_tree`), which is every tiled fit written before those planes were recorded. Without them the viewer orders parts by content centroid, which is not a valid painter's order: it flips discretely as the camera moves, so `normal` and `volumetric` blending pop at the seams on every orbit. Where the parts are disjoint the planes are recoverable from the part boxes alone, so the doctor rebuilds them exactly — on the bundled zebrafish stack that turns 41 centroid-ordered parts into an ordering that satisfies all 74,186 pairwise constraints sampled over 160 camera poses, inside and outside the volume.

It also catches the harder version of the same problem: a `bsp_tree` that is *present but disagrees* with where the parts actually sit, left behind in a pre-transform coordinate space or written against a different part set. That is worse than a missing tree, because the traversal still returns a plausible permutation and the ordering is confidently wrong rather than falling back. Those are rebuilt when possible and otherwise removed, on the grounds that the centroid heuristic is at least honest.

Not everything is repairable, and the doctor says so rather than guessing. A uniform-tiled fit keeps each tile's apodization halo, so its parts genuinely intersect and no exact ordering exists to recover; that is reported with the remedy — re-fit — and left alone.

Adding a check means writing one function and appending it to a registry; see `packages/luxar/src/luxar/gsplats/doctor/README.md`.
