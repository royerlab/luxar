#### A stacked gsplat axis survives the default (lossy) center encoding

Saving a dataset built with `GSplatData.combine_as_new_dimension(..., sigma=0.0)`
silently destroyed the axis it had just created. That call floors the new axis's
sigma to 1e-7 — semantically right for a discrete time/channel axis, which has no
physical extent — while `AUTO` (the default) and `MEMORY` store centers as per-axis
uint16 fixed point, whose step on an axis is `(hi - lo) / 65535`. For a hundred
stacked timepoints spanning 0..99 that step is 1.5e-3, so quantization can move a
center by 7.6e-4, or 7553 σ. Ninety-six of the hundred frames landed off their
integer coordinate and stopped matching any slice query. The failure was invisible
on inspection because uint16 quantization is exact at the bounds, so the first and
last frame of a timelapse always looked correct.

The gsplat array writer now carries a **sigma rail** alongside the encoder's existing
extent rail. The extent rail sees only the coordinates, and a stacked axis has a
perfectly reasonable extent — it is the splats' own width that is impossibly small.
`write_gsplat_arrays` is the one place that holds `centers` and `cholesky_factors`
at the same time (both the scene compiler and the standalone `.gsplats.zarr` tree
writer funnel through it), so the check lives there. Per axis, a splat counts as
*unrepresentable* when the worst-case round-trip displacement — half the grid step —
exceeds `MAX_CENTER_DISPLACEMENT_SIGMAS` (1.0) times that splat's own marginal σ, and
the centers fall back to float32 when more than `MAX_UNREPRESENTABLE_SPLAT_FRACTION`
(0.1%) of the splats are unrepresentable on some axis. The warning names the axis, the
step, the displacement bound, how many splats are affected and their median σ. A
degenerate axis with σ exactly 0 is unrepresentable at any step; a constant axis, whose
step is 0, can never be. The marginal σ is read straight off the packed
lower-triangular rows (`Σ[i,i] = Σ_{j≤i} L[i,j]²`) in one `N·k` pass, without
materializing the `(N, d, d)` matrices.

Both numbers are keyed on **harm**, not on jitter. A center moved by less than one σ
is still inside the core the splat was fitted to describe, with the displacement
bounded by the splat's own width rather than by the axis extent, so it renders
essentially where it did. That is not a claim that every query answer survives — a
slice query matches out to roughly `tolerance + coverage_sigma·σ`, so a splat right on
that boundary and pushed the permitted 1.0 σ outward drops out of it, and because the
test is *per axis* a 4-D center may move a full σ on all four at once (2 σ in
Mahalanobis terms for a diagonal Σ) with every per-axis check passing. What the bound
does rule out is the failure this rail is for: past ~1 σ the center leaves that
footprint altogether, which is how a stacked frame ends up thousands of σ from its
integer coordinate and vanishes. A tighter
per-splat line charges 2× the centers bytes for sub-voxel error — measured on 200,000
splats spread over an 8192-voxel axis with 5% pinned at the fitter's
`sqrt(1/12) ≈ 0.2887` σ floor, the step is 0.125 voxel, so the worst displacement is
0.0625 voxel = 0.217 σ; that is 12.9% of splats "unrepresentable" at a quarter-σ line
and 0.03% at this one.

Because the per-splat line is strict, the population gate can be loose no longer. Under
the displacement criterion the benign populations collapse — worst measured axis: 0.015%
for a 20,000-splat random-`U(0, 1)`-Cholesky fixture, 0.0005% for a 200,000-splat
SPZ-like log-normal scale distribution (0.001% with an added σ=1e-6 needle), 0.03% for
the light-sheet case above — so 0.1% still clears every one of them by 3× or more while
catching what a 1% gate missed: a degenerate *minority*. Merging a 2,000-splat
`sigma=0` track stack into a 300,000-splat fit with a real `σ_t = 3.0` leaves 0.662% of
the splats displaced by up to 1,373 σ, and under the old gate the rail stayed silent
while only 10% of the tracks still landed on their own frame. The population test is
still a population test, not a minimum: real fits routinely contain a handful of needle
Gaussians — an SPZ import decodes scales as `exp(u8/16 - 10)` with a 4.5e-5 floor — and
a single such splat must not double every centers array on disk (measured: 3.18 MB →
6.23 MB of centers, +54% on a 600k-splat store). The limitation that buys is real and
worth stating: up to 0.1% of a store can still be quantized away with no warning at all
— 1,650 splats on a 1.65M-splat fit, or 100 `sigma=0` tracks merged into a 150,000-splat
time-axis fit, 61 of which end up off their own frame while the rail stays silent.
Displacement magnitude cannot separate those two populations (an ordinary `U(0, 1)`
Cholesky draw yields σ = 1e-9 and thousands of σ of displacement), so a small tracked
sub-population that must land exactly on its coordinate should be saved with
`EncodingMode.PRECISION` rather than left to the rail. Note too that a *fraction*
tightens at small N: one unrepresentable splat trips the whole array whenever N ≤ 999.

An escalated centers array is written with `deduplicate=False`. The encoder's
content-dedup registry is keyed on the centers **bytes**, but the rail's verdict depends
on a *sibling* array (`cholesky_factors`) that the registry does not key on — so two
nodes with byte-identical centers and different Cholesky collapsed onto whichever was
written first, and the escalated node was stored as a `(0, d)` `array_ref` pointing at
the other node's `linear_perchannel_u16` array. It then read back quantized: the
original bug verbatim, behind a warning claiming it had been prevented. Skipping the
registry is the whole fix, since `ArrayRefRegistry.check` both looks up and registers,
so an escalated array that never calls it neither resolves to a lossy target nor becomes
one.

Only the **centers** escalate. The Cholesky, amplitude and color tiers keep whatever
the caller asked for — the published cell-tracking bundle depends on the Cholesky
staying uint8, and inflating it 4× would buy nothing.

The sigma rail also stands down once an axis reaches `COORDINATE_U16_MAX_EXTENT`
(2¹⁶, now single-sourced in `typing_utils.constants` so the two rails cannot drift).
The extent rail already stores such an array as float32, exactly, and says why —
"extent ≥ 2¹⁶; uint16 fixed-point cannot resolve a unit step" — which is the useful
diagnosis for a scene that is merely very large. Firing here as well would replace it
with one pointing at a degenerate stacked axis that is not there, and would drop the
array's content dedup for a verdict that depends on the centers bytes alone.

Four 4D demos (`cell_tracking_challenge`, `celegans_tracking`, `nexrad_supercell`,
`zebrafish_timelapse`) also cached each per-timepoint fit under `MEMORY` and then
stacked the *in-memory* object rather than what had just been written, while their
cache-hit branch loaded the store back. A cold run and a warm run therefore stacked
different splats. Each now returns what it stored, so the two runs agree — and both
branches load with `include_stats=True`, so the fit's normalization provenance (the
background floor of #1175) still reaches the stacked scene through
`agreed_normalization_stats`.
