#### LOD recipe guidance now covers first-paint request cost

The `luxar-gsplat-pipeline` skill's recipe table was purely scale-ordered —
`tiles` for large N, `overview` for huge N, `adaptive` for largest N — and said
nothing about what a topology costs in HTTP requests before the viewer paints
anything. Read as a ladder, it pointed an author at `adaptive` for a 1.58 GiB 4D
store on the strength of its byte size alone, producing a store that needs ~689
requests to first paint where the same data as one stacked leaf with a `stream`
ladder needs 39.

A new subsection states the rule that actually decides this. First paint pays
`eager parts × 1 level × min(3, rungs per level)` eager rungs, each costing a
handful of requests — one per array plus one per extra chunk — because a
`kind=lod` group loads only its `default_level` and defers the rest while a
`kind=partition` group loads every part eagerly. That splits the recipes where
the scale ordering does not: `overview` is the one large-N recipe whose first
paint is independent of part count, while `tiles` and `adaptive` scale with it,
and `levels` promotes off its eager level on the first selector frame anyway.
`luxar gsplat lod --help` carries the short form.

The measured 15–18× gap is recorded alongside the honest limits: re-chunking
moves first paint 25–37% at fixed structure but cannot touch the structure term,
and the observed requests-per-node ratio spans 7.9×, so no fitted constant may be
carried forward.
