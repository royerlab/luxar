#### Depth sorting survives a slow SortWorker start

On a large scene the viewer could stop depth-sorting everything, silently, for the rest of the session. At around three million points — `demo_gaia_milky_way_3m` is exactly this shape — the SortWorker's one-time `initialize()` missed its 30 s deadline, and the coordinator cached that rejection forever. Every order-dependent layer, `normal` and `volumetric` alike and across all four geometry types, then drew in storage order until the page was reloaded.

The deadline was measuring the wrong thing. The worker was spawned lazily by the first order-dependent commit, which is precisely when this thread and the data-worker pool are saturated decoding the scene — and the worker's reply has to be dispatched on that same busy thread. In the reproduction the worker logged its own readiness (WASM up, answer sent) while the main thread timed out and terminated it.

Two changes. The worker is now started at app init, while nothing is loading, so initialization no longer overlaps the data flood. And a missed deadline is treated as transient rather than fatal: the worker keeps running with its `initialize()` still in flight, and the per-frame scheduler retries once the loader goes idle, re-awaiting the same call and re-registering every tracked node. An `onerror` death or an explicit `initialize()` rejection is still permanent, and after three consecutive deadline misses it gives up — but no longer quietly: the data-loading monitor's performance footer now reads `depth sort UNAVAILABLE`.

The deadline is configurable as `depthSort.workerInitTimeoutMs`, and the coordinator now shares the data-worker pool's init guard instead of hand-rolling its own, which also gains it the `onmessageerror` arm it was missing.
