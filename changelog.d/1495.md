#### Thin capsule lines keep their whole leg at a sharp bend (#1495)

The joint deficit packet — the term that fills in the light a plain bisector
cut would chop when the partner leg cannot cover my foreign side — was skipped
below a 4 px stencil half-width, on the rationale that a deficit at a hairline
joint is sub-pixel. It is not, once the bend is sharp: past 120° of turn the
bisector tilts toward the leg's own axis and cuts its rod LENGTHWISE, so a
short or hairpin partner left part of a leg unrendered anywhere along it.
Measured on the CPU joint model with a short partner (`ql = 0.4 r`), the plain
cut deviates −0.28 of peak at 135° and −0.53 at 160° at r = 1.5 px, and −0.35 /
−0.72 at r = 3.5 px, against −0.11/−0.12 and −0.05/−0.05 for a congruent joint
at the same angle and radius. Thin polylines with sharp direction changes now
render whole.

The width gate itself stays, so nothing changes for the common case, and the
exception to it is doubly narrow: the turn must be sharper than 120° — the same
axis-dot test the packet gate's own sharp clause uses, rather than a second
threshold — and BOTH of the segment's raw radii must reach the 1.5 px AA
radius floor. Below that floor the fragment's thin-width energy compensation
dims a leg while the deficit still cancels the partner's unscaled profile, so a
pair at different scales OVER-brightens (+0.14…+0.16 of peak measured at 175°
with one end sub-floor) — a bead, which is the artifact class this primitive
exists to remove. That is a real trade, not a free win: a drawable sub-floor
taper would compose better WITH a packet (−0.05…−0.07 against −0.33…−0.79
gated), and we take the dim gap over the bright bead. Nor does the floor make
the deficit under-fill-only: the near-fade term can still differ between two
legs sharing a vertex, a pre-existing hole this change does not close.

The cost is fragment work, not stencil area: a live packet makes every
foreign-side fragment evaluate the partner rod instead of discarding, and past
120° that region runs lengthwise along the rod — 18.3% of a leg's own covered
footprint at 121°, 27.5% at 150°, 56.8% at 175° (r = 2 px), against 12–15% at a
gentle joint. The thin-line fill arm was not re-run, and the perf benches are
not obviously immune: their random-walk generator draws each step
independently, so 24.9% of their joints turn past 120°.

Three residuals are accepted deliberately, all pinned by the unit sweep: a
joint at or below 120° still hard-cuts (−0.19 of peak at `ql = 0.4 r`, growing
to −0.39 as the partner shortens); the angle threshold is hard, so a projected
bend sweeping through it pops (−0.175 → 0.000 across 119.5° → 120.5°); and the
radius floor is a harder pop still (−0.21 → 0.000 at 125°, −0.72 → 0.000 at
175°, across raw 1.4999 → 1.5 px) that a plain zoom sweeps through. All four
vertex surfaces — both material backends, both picking backends — carry the
same condition, so the pick shape still tracks the drawn pixels.
