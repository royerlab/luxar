#### The cryo-EM capsid was framed as a texture wall

The gallery tile for the giant virus capsid was unreadable: the subject filled
the frame edge to edge and the auto-exposure meter, seeing a frame with almost no
background, pulled it down to a dark muddy brown. You could not tell it was a
capsid.

The capture harness had detected it and said so precisely at capture time —
`cropped? 3047/4316 border pixels (70.6% of the frame perimeter) are lit`, worst
case across all ten poses, with the fix named — but a warning nothing reads does
not stop a publish, and the tile shipped.

`fillTarget` drops to 0.65. Measured across the series: border-lit 70.6% at the
default 0.95, then 34.2%, 17.7% and 15.2% at 0.85/0.75/0.65. The harness keeps
suggesting a lower value at every step because a dense icosahedron always clips
its own silhouette, so the value was chosen on the render rather than on the
warning clearing — at 0.65 the hexagonal outline, the capsomer texture and the
tail stalk are all legible. Auto-exposure is the other half and follows the
framing for free: −1.06 stops at 0.95 against +2.33 at 0.65.

Worth recording that the coverage metric preferred the broken framing — 93% at
0.95 against 63% at 0.65 — because its percentile bounding box ignores exactly
the outliers that were touching the frame edge. Coverage cannot see this class of
fault; the border-lit measurement is what catches it.

Also worth separating from fit quality: at 19.2 dB foreground PSNR this is among
the four lowest-scoring hosted archives, yet `opencell_map4` renders well at
18.0 dB. The metric was never what made this tile look bad, and a refit would
have produced the same dark wall.
