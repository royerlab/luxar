#### 4D zebrafish timelapse: one node, seventeen times the splats, and a cage to read it against

The zebrafish gastrulation demo wrote one flat gsplats node per timepoint,
pinned to its frame with `fill={"time": t}` — 64 sibling nodes, no level of
detail, no shared appearance and a Layers row each. It now stacks the
per-timepoint fits into a single 4D `GSplatData` whose fourth centre column is
time, and gives that node a substitutive LOD ladder in which time is a hard
coarsening barrier, so a coarse level never blends one frame's cells into the
next. Timepoints are still fitted independently and cached one by one; only the
thing that is shipped and displayed changed.

The fit itself was starving, in two independent ways.

It asked for 2,000 seeds and then took the fitter's bare defaults — 1,000
iterations, below what the `draft` preset would give, and `cull_retention=0.95`,
which throws away most of the splats along with the last 5% of amplitude. A busy
timepoint shipped a few hundred splats, and whole cells were missing from the
render. A measured sweep put the plateau at ~13,000 splats (32,000 seeds, 5,000
iterations, retention 0.9999); doubling the seeds again moves the
reconstruction by 0.13 dB, because the cull and not the seed budget is what sets
the count on data this heavy-tailed.

And it was subtracting a background floor that was not background. `--floor
auto` takes the histogram mode of the NON-ZERO voxels, capped at their median —
correct where the non-zero population is a camera pedestal, and inverted here,
where 98.7–99.8% of voxels are exactly zero so that population is the specimen
itself. The level landed at 26/255 at the first timepoint and climbed to 91/255
as the embryo brightened, deleting between a quarter and 56% of the image
energy before fitting began. Turning it off is worth up to +7.6 dB global and
+10.7 dB foreground.

Together with the budget, the fit this demo now does is +6.8 dB global and
+10.2 dB foreground on the frame both were measured on. It also fits all 151
timepoints at full XY resolution — the old stride arithmetic took 64 frames and
stopped at frame 126, so the last 50 minutes of gastrulation were never in the
demo at all.

The shipped archive is now one 22.3 MB `.gsplats.zarr.zip` holding **1,590,010
splats** over all 151 timepoints in four substitutive levels (24k / 98k / 397k /
1.59M), replacing a 1.6 MB bundle of 64 per-frame zips. Measured over the whole
archive from the fits' own stamps: a median of 9,144 splats per timepoint (range
6,901–18,893), median global PSNR 45.60 dB (36.60–50.39) and median foreground
PSNR 20.95 dB (18.68–23.38).

Because the labelled endoderm occupies under 2% of the imaged voxels, the scene
now draws the acquisition volume as a toggleable wireframe cage ruled every
100 µm. The Time slider reads in minutes, from the LSM's own 120 s frame
interval, and the acquisition geometry is checked against the file on the refit
path rather than assumed.

Three things only the viewer showed. The Time axis steps by exactly 2.0 min
rather than the recorded 2.0001470947: a discrete dimension becomes an
`<input type="range">` whose value snaps onto `min + k*step`, and with the raw
interval the browser clamps the final timepoint out of reach — 1.3 s of drift
over five hours buys an axis whose last stop exists. The `Dimensions` list is
ordered X, Y, Z, because that order is what the viewer maps onto screen x/y/z
and listing Z first showed an 859 × 859 × 316 µm slab edge-on as a tall narrow
column. And the cage's colours were roughly halved, because additive lines over
a specimen filling 2% of the frame out-shone the thing they exist to measure.

`--show-roundtrip` is gone. It rendered splats fitted in physical coordinates
onto a voxel-indexed grid, so every PSNR it printed was measuring a coordinate
mismatch rather than the fit. The per-timepoint quality the fitter stamps for
itself is reported instead.
