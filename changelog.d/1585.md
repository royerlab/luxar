#### A 3x3 matrix of tracked zebrafish embryos, from the Biohub cell-tracking challenge

`luxar demo run gsplats_4d_cell_tracking_challenge` builds nine crops of a
developing zebrafish embryo — the most densely annotated crops of the public
[Biohub cell-tracking competition](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/biohub-cell-tracking-during-development)
data — into one 3x3 matrix scene. Each tile carries a full 100-timepoint
timelapse in three geometries at once: the image data as a 4D (ZYX + time)
Gaussian-splat volume, every annotated cell position as points that appear only
at the timepoint they belong to, and every tracking link as one `indexed` lines
node whose shared vertices make a cell division render as a real fork. Colour is
lineage throughout. Scrub time and the markers walk along their own tracks.

`luxar.gsplats.interop.geff` reads **GEFF** cell-lineage graphs — the tracking
interchange format used by ultrack, trackedit, tracksdata and traccuracy — into a
plain-NumPy `TrackingGraph`, with physical-unit positions, connected-component
lineage ids for colouring, division detection, and edges remapped to positional
indices so a lineage forest drops straight into `scene.add_lines`. No `geff`
package, no `networkx`; a GEFF store is a zarr v3 group, which the `zarr>=3.2`
the project now depends on reads directly.

Two authoring choices are measured rather than guessed, and both matter more than
they sound. The per-timepoint fits drop the top 5% of splats by characteristic
size: a fit of densely packed nuclei leaves a diffuse large-scale tail, and under
volumetric blending optical depth grows with path length, so that few percent
renders as opaque discs burying the nuclei underneath.

And the LOD ladder's depth is the frame budget. The `screen-area` selector
anchors its finest level at half the screen and halves per step, so a tile of a
3x3 grid (0.0855 of the screen area) draws the COARSEST level whatever the
ladder's length — one knob therefore sets both what the opening framing costs and
how much detail it throws away. A one-level ladder drew 20,072 splats per
timepoint per crop, 140,504 per frame across the matrix, and scrubbing time was
visibly choppy. The shipped four-level ladder draws 4,885 per timepoint per crop
— a 4.1x cut — and the viewer never even fetches the finer levels. That costs
nothing visible: rendering the drawn level back to a volume shows quality
saturating around 5,000 splats per timepoint, while masked PSNR is useless here,
flat at 19.7-19.8 dB across a 37x range in count because merging preserves the
low-frequency envelope it scores. Zooming into one embryo still climbs to the
full four-million-splat fit.

Track lines are thin (0.35 um) and translucent (opacity 0.15): they are context
for the volume, not the subject, and at full width and opacity a matrix tile is
mostly cage. Track width and marker radius are re-derived from constants at load
rather than read back from the hosted payload — both are uniform, so storing them
would pin the demo's appearance to a 0.68 GB artifact and make retuning a
re-upload.

The splat budget comes from measurement too. `gsplat cal` puts the held-out peak
at K* = 12,126, but that is the noise-masked criterion; against a scale-invariant
PSNR over the nuclei alone the fidelity climbs ~1 dB per doubling with no plateau
(37.4 dB at 9.4k splats/timepoint, 39.3 at 42.6k, 40.3 at 83.3k), and the
difference is plain in a MIP. The default is 60,000 seeds, keeping ~43k.

Fitting the whole matrix is ~900 per-timepoint fits (~24 s each on an RTX PRO
6000), cached per timepoint, so the demo is resumable and a second run is cheap.
The competition data is behind an authenticated endpoint, so the demo declares
`local_data: "kaggle-auth"`, gates the Kaggle client through `require_module`,
and backs off when Kaggle rate-limits a bulk fetch. The redistributable derived
product (per-crop 4D volumes plus track geometry, ~700 MB) is registered in
`data_manifest.json` as a `zenodo` dataset so a warm run needs neither
credentials nor a GPU.

`scripts/zenodo_upload_draft.py` adds files to an unpublished Zenodo draft and
is deliberately incapable of publishing: there is no publish call in it, a
submitted deposition aborts the run, dry run is the default, and every upload is
verified against the checksum Zenodo computes.
