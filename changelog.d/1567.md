#### The capsule joint's bounded partner rod is now pinned by tests, not just by code

The capsule line primitive's joint deficit rule rebuilds the partner leg from a
packed packet — a radius gradient in `cut.z`, the partner's projected length in
`cut.w` — and renders `max(mine − partner, 0)` on the partner's side of the
bisector. #1490 was that the reconstruction ignored `cut.w` on the profile side:
an unbounded rod, with no far cap, subtracted far past where the partner
actually ends. That defect is already fixed in all four capsule shader surfaces
(the GLSL visual and pick fragments, and their two TSL twins) and in the CPU
mirror; this change adds no behaviour, it closes the hole that let the fix be
deleted again in silence. Deleting the far cap's overshoot term from the four
shaders while leaving the mirror alone keeps every other test in the viewer
unit suite green.

Two pieces of coverage close it. A source lock
(`tests/unit/rendering/materials/line/capsule-joint-packet-source-lock.test.ts`)
pins, across all four shader surfaces, the bounded reconstruction, both packet
lanes and the packing that transports them, the partner call and its per-end
operands, and the four packet-gate clauses (#1495 / #1501); it also checks that
the CPU mirror carries the same bounded rod, and that no fifth surface has
appeared unlocked. Everything matches on comment-stripped text, so prose can
never satisfy a pin. A parity fixture pair
(`line-capsule-joint-short-partner` and its pick twin) renders a joint whose
partner ends well inside the joint disc — the regime the far cap exists for,
which no previous capsule fixture reached — so both backends actually execute
the term.

Adding the pair also turned up a latent false green next door: the existing
`line-capsule-pick-joint` fixture rendered with the depth test off on the WebGL
side while its WebGPU twin had it on, so the two backends could resolve
different pick winners and still land under the parity bound. It now opts into
the harness's `depthCompete` flag like the new pick entry.

The source lock is the unit-test half; the parity harness runs in the dedicated
`tsl-parity` CI job, where the fixture pair remains the only thing that can catch
a divergence in just one backend.
