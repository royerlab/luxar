#### Bloom matches between the WebGL and WebGPU renderers again

On three's WebGL2 fallback backend — `?renderer=webgpu` when no WebGPU adapter or
device is available, and the `?webgpuForceWebgl` diagnostic flag — the bloom
pyramid's first frame after each build came out wrong along Y: a vertical gradient
in the glow lost most of its slope, while a radially symmetric one looked right. A
real WebGPU session was never affected: the render-target Y-flip uniform this rides
on is only created when `builder.isFlipY()` is true, which `GLSLNodeBuilder` returns
and `WGSLNodeBuilder` does not. The parity harness measured the composed
three-level pyramid at 16.4 mean absolute levels of difference (per covered pixel)
on a vertical ramp and 0.000 on a Gaussian blob, with the ramp's fitted gradient
falling from 4.95 to 1.25 levels per row.

The bloom TSL factories bind one texture node per pass and re-point it at a different
mip between passes. Each of the four box-filter taps is a _clone_ of that node, and
each clone derives its own render-target Y-flip from the bound texture — three.js's way
of presenting WebGPU's top-down framebuffer convention on its WebGL fallback backend.
The re-point was happening inside the node-update phase, so the taps that were already
updated derived their flip from the _previous_ texture: one tap sampled the mip
un-mirrored while the other three sampled it mirrored, and the box average collapsed to
half the vertical gradient about its own mean. That signature — a shrunken slope with
the mean untouched — is why a frame-average check never noticed. Binding the texture in
`updateBefore` instead, which the renderer runs for the whole graph ahead of any update,
makes every tap agree.

The mismatch needs the previous texture to differ from the new one in
`isRenderTargetTexture`, which in practice means the _first_ frame of each bloom
session: from the second frame on, every tap already agrees. A resize does not start
a new session — it only reallocates the mip targets, leaving the three materials and
their bound nodes in place — so the builds that pay for it are enabling bloom from a
disabled state (including the first enable) and a WebGL context restore. That is still
worth fixing, since every session that shows bloom pays it once, and it is why the
identical binding in the FXAA pass, whose host also swaps a `null`
placeholder for a render target on its first render, was moved to `updateBefore` too.
Both now share one `bindLiveTexture` helper that carries the reasoning with it.

The composed-pyramid parity case now runs twice, on the Gaussian and on a vertical
ramp. The ramp case is the one with the spatial teeth: it asserts per-row means and
a fitted gradient — including its sign and its magnitude — rather than only the frame
average the Gaussian case still checks. It also feeds the chain a render-target texture the way
production does, instead of a raw `DataTexture` plus a row flip on the way out — a raw
data texture is the one input the two backends genuinely disagree about, and flipping
the result afterwards would have hidden any real mirror regression along with the
artificial one.
