#### Two glass surfaces that fought their own theme

Two panels violated the glass-surface constraints the UI design guide spells
out in §5.1, and both showed it only under liquid-glass. The toast faded
`opacity` on its own glass root, so the refraction and tint layers rode the
fade out with the badge instead of staying behind it. The dimension-sliders
panel scrolled on its glass root, and a scroll container clips the very layers
that paint at `inset: 0` behind it.

The toast is no longer a glass surface at all. A two-second badge whose dismiss
animation *is* an opacity fade belongs with the REC pill and the cursor context
menus — framed with the badge tier of the panel recipe, but not glassed — so the
fade is now legal rather than tolerated, and liquid-glass paints it the same
dark tint those siblings already get (that theme's `--luxar-bg-secondary` is a
translucent white, which would leave white text on a bright render). The
dimension sliders delegate scrolling to an inner `__scroll` wrapper like every
other panel, keep their glass root at `overflow: visible`, and move from the
baseline in-canvas layer to the dropdown tier their placement implies.

One surface in this class is still open and is now named as such in the guide's
debt inventory: the resolution indicator ramps `opacity` on its own glass root
via its fade-in/fade-out keyframes, and wants the same treatment — an inner
wrapper, or the toast's route out of the glass system.
