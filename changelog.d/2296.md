#### Reduce the opening load of the Cosmicflows Laniakea demo

The full preset gives each basin a **streaming (additive) ladder**, so the nine
basin nodes paint progressively instead of arriving all at once. At the `full`
preset a basin runs to ~617,685 vertices, three times the 200,000 at which
`scripts/check_demo_ladders.py` requires a ladder, and these nodes had none.

This first landed as a *substitutive* ladder, and the measurement taken for it is
what argued against it. A whole-object ladder's finest anchor is fixed at 0.5
screen occupancy, adding levels cannot move it, and explicit
`coverage_fractions` would revert the group to the legacy diagonal-coverage
selector. At the authored opening pose two substitutive levels settled at ~892k
splats on a 16:9 viewport — but the wide outer basins still selected ~2.1M and
~3.2M *fine* segments at 4:3 and 1:1. The aspect ratio, not the ladder, decided
whether the scene was bounded. A prefix ladder bounds first paint at every aspect
ratio because it does not depend on level selection at all.

It also keeps the geometry: `substitutive_lod=` coarsens a line set by
**synthesising gsplats** (strings of beads), and on this demo the streamline
ribbons are the picture. The additive path preserves whole streamlines.

Indexed lines could not carry an additive ladder when the substitutive version
was written — the framework refused the whole `line_type`. It now verifies
instead: the multi-LOD writer rebuilds edges by chaining each connected component
in ascending vertex order, which is faithful exactly when every component already
is an ascending simple path, and this layout qualifies.
