#### LOD ladders and `gsplat info` read the dataset's own truncation radius

Gaussian splats carry the σ support they were fitted at and are rendered at
(`truncation_radius`, canonically 2.75), but the LOD path kept a parallel knob
that never consulted the data: `compute_additive_order`, `make_additive_lod`,
`make_lod_pyramid` and `RecipeParams` all defaulted `truncation_sigmas` to a
hardcoded 3.0, so the sparse-Gram pruning that decides which splat pairs
interact was computed at a support the dataset never had. Those defaults are now
`None`, meaning "the dataset's own `truncation_radius`", resolved through a
single shared helper from the object actually being pruned — for
`make_additive_lod` that is the selected substitutive level, not the finest leaf,
since per-level radii round-trip independently. An explicit value still
overrides, and is checked locally against what this σ actually is (a finite
positive CPU pruning cutoff, deliberately *not* the render pipeline's float32
bounds), so a degenerate `--truncation-sigmas 0` fails loudly instead of
silently emptying the Gram while a tiny-but-real `1e-5` still runs. The two
private helpers that consume the number (`_truncation_radii`,
`_build_sparse_gram`) require it as a keyword with no default, so the old bug
cannot be re-introduced by a new caller forgetting to pass it.

`luxar gsplat info` had the same literal in its volume report: it measured every
splat's extent at 3σ and labelled the table `VOLUME ANALYSIS (3-Sigma)`
regardless of the file. It now measures and labels at the dataset's radius —
`(3/2.75)³ ≈ 1.3`, so a canonically-fitted 3D dataset's reported volumes were
30% too large. The volume formula itself is unchanged.

A dataset fitted at 3.0 sees no change. One fitted at anything else gets a
ladder pruned (and a report measured) at its real support, so ordering and
energy-fraction cutpoints can shift slightly — for the better, since they now
match what the viewer draws.

The wider direction costs time, and that is the price of the accuracy. Under the
truncation convention a pair whose supports do not overlap is *exactly* zero, so
pruning a 6σ store at 3σ was discarding genuinely non-zero pairs; honouring the
data restores them, and there are a lot of them. On 3000 uniformly scattered 3D
splats the sparse Gram grows from 181k non-zeros at 3σ to 1.13M at 6σ and 3.68M
at 10σ — roughly the cubic growth of the support volume — and an end-to-end
`make_additive_lod(method="greedy")` on the same data goes from 4.2 s at 3σ to
20.0 s at 6σ. The value is deliberately not clamped: a cap would simply reinstate
the accuracy bug for exactly the wide-support datasets that need the wider Gram.

`luxar gsplat decimate` inherits the change too, and it is worth calling out
because nothing in its own signature mentions σ: the `prefix` family calls
`compute_additive_order` with no knob of its own, so on non-3.0 data the exact
set of splats it keeps and discards moves with the ordering. The drift measured
small — on 400 dense splats at 2.75σ vs 3.0σ the two greedy permutations agree
for the first several dozen ranks and most prefixes keep the same set — but it is
not nil, so a re-run of `decimate` on such a dataset will not be bit-identical to
one from before this change.
